# The setting: the world, its time, and a house inside it

> **What belongs here:** what the world is made of, how its time works, and what a house is inside it — the
> fiction. **What does not:** what the player does with it (`MECHANICS.md`), what the engine behind it looks
> like (`ARCHITECTURE.md`), what is being built next (`ROADMAP.md`).

It records what Adam has described so that it is not lost between conversations, and it marks what is settled
and what is not. Where it speaks in his terms — house-spirit, key figure, power state — those are the words,
and they are in the glossary.

Reviewed 2026-10-20, when the factions were given a reason to be everywhere, the gods a reason to feed them, and
the first of them, the Vigil, was described. Reviewed 2026-10-17, when the house was found to be one of the
energies itself, the gods alien, and the guides described. Reviewed 2026-10-16, when the story was told in order for the first time, in `STORY.md`, and settled who broke the
gods' plan. Reviewed 2026-09-20, when the age and the gods were written down for the first time. Reviewed 2026-09-19. Three things this document used to assert are gone, and the rest of the repository has
been corrected to match: play is chronological, so a session is no longer a visit to a chosen century; there
are no pins, so nothing obliges the past to have been a particular way; and the roll engine, renamed the
**fate engine**, is described in `ARCHITECTURE.md` rather than here.

## The world

A warped Earth, as the map already is, and a warped history to go with it: NPC factions and famous
characters drawn from real history and bent the way the coastlines are bent. The game takes place over
**centuries**. A player does not play one moment of it.

**The world opened on the first of January, 1790**, and it keeps Earth's calendar and Earth's years: the
months, the leap years and the count are all the ones a reader already knows. A warped Earth is still an
Earth, and a date in it should need no conversion.

## The age

**An enlightenment, interrupted.**

Reason is spreading and has been for a while: instruments, presses, academies, letters between people who
will never meet. It is not a clean break from what came before. Castles still stand and are still worth
holding; a cathedral is still the largest thing for a hundred miles. The new is growing up through the old
rather than replacing it, which is what an enlightenment actually looks like from inside one.

And it is being interrupted. **The social order that held is not holding.** The towns and cities have fallen,
every one that stood in 1785 and the greatest worst: some burned, some were emptied by the people who fled them,
some simply came apart, and by 1790 they are **ruins that people have learnt to shun** - a great city's for miles,
a market town's a few roofless houses. Riches lie in them still, and there are always some who go to fetch them,
but nobody goes without risk, and nobody can say quite what the risk is. Where the order broke, **new factions
rose into the gap**: whatever can organise people now does, and some of what can is nothing anybody would have
chosen.

To everyone living through it this is a calamity with no author. A bad century. A run of failures. Judgement,
or somebody's fault, or the weather. Every explanation the world offers is wrong.

## The factions

**Where the order broke, factions rose into the gap, and they are everywhere.** There are five, one for each of
the five things a house can have - wealth, domain, influence, reason and piety - and each is an NPC power with a
history of its own, the same body the whole world over, its face changing with the ground it stands on as a
structure's does. Settled on 2026-10-20 with the first of them, the Vigil; the other four are sketches. What a
house does with a faction is `MECHANICS.md`'s.

### Why they are everywhere

**The head fell, the body walked on.** Every great body of the old world kept its head in a city: the Curia in
Rome, the Porte in Constantinople, the Companies' courts in London and Amsterdam, the academies, the courts of the
crowns. When the cities fell, the heads fell with them. But the bodies had never lived in the cities. They were
the abbeys in their valleys, the fleets at sea, the stewards on the estates, the correspondents in their country
houses and the couriers on the roads, and the ritual weighed least where minds were few, so the bodies were
spared. Each grew a new head in open country, and found the others along the lines that had always joined them:
the sea lanes, the pilgrim roads, the post.

That is why a faction is a thing of the whole world when nothing else in 1790 is. The cities fell everywhere at
once, so the same gap opened everywhere at once, and everywhere the same five kinds of survivor stood at its
edge. It had happened before, smaller: when the Carolingian order came apart about the year 1000, it was Cluny's
web of abbeys and the Peace of God that held the countryside together. Why they rose so fast is the gods' doing,
and a secret: *What feeds the factions*, below.

### The five

Proposed on 2026-10-20, and only the last is more than a sketch. Each is designed in its own turn.

- **Wealth: the Company.** The fleets that were at sea in 1785 came home to burning ports and were the only thing
  left that could move grain from where it grew to where it was wanted, so they pooled their hulls and their
  debts. Drawn from the East India Companies, the Hanse's kontors, the Armenian traders of New Julfa and the
  Hokkien junk trade.
- **Domain: the stewards.** The estates, their granaries and the improving societies that fed the countryside
  when the towns could not. Not yet named.
- **Influence: the courts in exile.** Kings without capitals keeping court in the country, and the posts that
  carry their letters and so decide what any of them knows. Drawn from the émigré courts of the French
  Revolution and Thurn und Taxis's imperial post.
- **Reason: the Republic of Letters.** The correspondents on their country estates, the observatories on their
  hills and the academies scattered when their cities fell, writing to each other still.
- **Piety: the Vigil**, a secret society behind every faith.

### The Vigil

**Piety's faction is a secret society, and not a faith.** The faiths themselves are not factions. They are the
ground the Vigil stands in: real, every one of them, headless now and scattered, their holy cities shunned ruins
like every other city, their village priests and imams and monks carrying on as they can. Piety is still piety as
the age understands it, observance and standing with one's church. The Vigil is the hand inside that glove: a
brotherhood hidden in every faith, which no faith knows the whole of and none would own, and which no player begins
knowing is there: it finds them. **No faith is the faction, and none is judged.** Adam's word of 2026-10-20. *The Vigil* is a working name; *the Wakeful* and *the Silence*
were the others.

**It was underground before the fall, which is why the fall could not take its head.** It is knotted from the
hidden chains every faith already kept in 1785: the Jesuits, suppressed in 1773 and living on in Russia and
Prussia; the Illuminati of Bavaria, banned in 1785, the year of the ritual, who gave their members and their
cities secret names; the Gold- und Rosenkreuzer; the Sufi orders' chains of teaching, hand to hand from master to
pupil, and the Naqshbandi's silent remembrance; the White Lotus in China, which already believed the age was
ending; the hidden Christians of Japan. Its shape it takes from them, and from two old beliefs that the world is
held up by a hierarchy nobody sees: the Sufis' Pole, Pillars and Substitutes, the Substitutes seven in many
tellings and named for being replaced when one dies, and the thirty-six hidden righteous of the Jews.

**What it shares is what its founders felt in 1785**: a weight laid on their minds from outside, which nobody
else alive noticed (*What feeds the factions* says why). That is the Vigil's own secret, and nobody outside it
knows it.

**How it is ordered.** A **lodge** by every great ruin, a **province** over each stretch of the world, and **the
Seven** over all of it; a lodge is what a Sufi order and a masonic one both call their house, and the Vigil means
both. Its members know the brothers and sisters of their own circle by their faces and the circle above only by
secret names, and the Seven are known to nobody below them. It recruits by sign and by letter, and never invites
a house it has not long watched. Its places are held by houses, as the protection of an abbey or a bishopric was
held by a noble family in the old world, with a key figure of the house sitting in it. It holds nothing in its
own name: whatever the Vigil commands, its houses hold.

**It quarrels**, over what the weight was. Three wings, a first draft:

- **The Penitents**: it was a judgement. Atone, and keep the faithful from the ruins.
- **The Pilgrims**: the holy places must be walked again. They lead the faithful into the ruins, and some come
  home changed.
- **The Listeners**: it came from outside, and it was not God. Find out what it was. The other two call it
  heresy, and it is the nearest to the truth.

## The gods

**This section is the truth, and no player begins knowing any of it.**

The gods are **energies, and they are not from here.** They came out of the far dark, from a nebula past any star
anyone has named, so long ago that they have forgotten it, and they have clung to what people are and do for so
long that they believe that is what they are: **aspects of what people are**, the things themselves rather than a
pantheon a culture invented and can un-invent. They wear whatever shape an age hands them. The Greek gods were one
such shape: a set of masks worn for a few centuries and then set down. They are not attached to those forms, they
do not answer to those names now, and a player who recognises one by its Greek face is recognising a costume. In
1790 the shape the age hands them is a human face.

**They took fright at the enlightenment, and they did something about it.** They feared reason would part
people from the soil, the seasons and the wild, and in 1785 they worked a ritual to turn every mind back toward
the green world by taking a little of each one's will. The apocalypse is what came of it. It was deliberate, it has
authors, and the authors are frightened rather than wicked - which is a different kind of antagonist and the
more interesting one.

**It is not going to plan.** They had traitors in their midst. Three of them - Oriel, Veyra and Merrin - would
not take a choice from anybody, liked the enlightenment besides, and broke the ritual from the inside: it bent
no mind, but it warped, and the world with it. It had borne down hardest where minds were thickest, so the warp
took hold in every town and city, deepest where the most minds had been, and poisoned the space there, which is
why they fell and why their ruins are shunned. Oriel died of it, and every god came out of it withered and
unsure of what had happened, so the disruption is now as chaotic for the gods as it is for anybody standing in
it. They are not a single will executing a design. They are a divided party half-way through something that is
already going wrong, improvising, and working against each other while they do it. The story of it, in order and
in its own voice, is `STORY.md`.

Oriel's energy flies outward still. One day it passes the place the gods came from, and what is there takes notice
and comes to see: a storyline of its own, for a server event far ahead, and not to be worked out yet.

### The houses are energies too

**The deepest secret is the player's own.** Not every energy that withered in 1785 was one of the council. Many
were too thin to hold to anything great, and to keep from fading they clung to a family - any family, it seems,
chosen by nothing - and have clung to it since, through births and deaths. **A house is one of them.** What the
player plays, the thing that outlives every key figure and never falls, is a withered energy holding on to a
bloodline, and it does not know what it is either.

That is what the house-spirit is, under what it does: why it never falls, why someone always rises when every key
figure dies - the energy clings to the next of them - and why a house's past is carried in it rather than in any of
its people. `VISION.md`'s *a house, not an avatar* is true inside the world.

### The guides

**The withered gods meet the houses in human form**, and that is how the story the player plays is told. A figure
comes into a house's life - a stranger at the gate, a guest who stays - and is a god wearing the face this age
hands it. **The player is never told.** They may see that the figure is not quite a person, and the game lets them:
an age that never moves while everyone else's counts, the same face in another house's hall under another name,
knowledge nobody living could have, a ruin that does not touch them.

**A guide wants something**, and acts on the shared world to get it by its own decision, as the NPCs do. At heart
every withered god wants to be strong again, and the ruins of the towns and cities, where the ritual's weight sank into
the streets, are the richest food there is. Every town of 1790 is a ruin, so wherever on the globe a house is
founded there is one within reach, and the ruin nearby is part of its guide's story.

Proposed on 2026-10-16 and not decided: **one guide to each great ruin, shared by every house founded within reach
of it, and every meeting with it the house's own.** Each meeting is offered to one house and walked on its own
history, so no house waits on another. What is shared is the guide itself, one face and one want, which every house
it guides feeds or starves, and its next move is chosen from all of them together, never from one player. A house
that has gone quiet costs nothing: the guide turns to whoever answers, and may say so, so a player's absence is the
guide's favour moving rather than a stalled story. Alone near a ruin, a shared guide is a personal one. Two players
who compare notes find the same face in both their halls, so the binding is a discovery and not a feature. Four
temperaments were proposed, each a storyline on the same want:

- **The Hungry**, a council god feeding on its ruin through the houses: the ruin grows cleaner and the houses
  richer, and the guide stronger and less kind.
- **The Hunter**, a council god looking for whoever broke the ritual, with the houses as its eyes, and slowly
  suspicious of them.
- **The Hidden**, Veyra or Merrin in disguise, who wants the ruins left alone and wills left free, and never
  pushes; Merrin's guidance travels as letters between houses.
- **The Lost**, a god that has forgotten what it is and asks the house to help it remember: the player's own
  secret, mirrored.

### What feeds the factions

**The factions rose faster than anything made by people rises, because the gods raised them.** Withered, their
ritual broken, each party of them clings now to the body of people that does what it is, and feeds it: a faction
is what a god has instead of the plan it lost. Those of holy dread hold the Vigil; those of the soil and the
seasons, the stewards, which makes them the gods' green creed in human form; Merrin, which loves words passed
between strangers, the Republic of Letters. Which gods hold the Company and the courts in exile is not decided.
Adam's word of 2026-10-20.

**That is why no faction stays weak for long.** When one is pushed down, the gods who cling to it pour into it
what little they have, and the world sees a revival, a run of omens, a winter that fills the chapels. When one
rises too high it overreaches, into a schism or a scandal. The world explains both its own way, and the contest
between the factions is the gods' quarrel fought by proxy, which nobody in the world knows.

**And it is why a house will not stay at the top of one.** A house is an energy too, and one sitting at a god's
own table is drinking from its well. How a house comes down, and what its spirit keeps of what it drank, is not
designed yet: tabled on 2026-10-20, so that it never feels bad for the player who climbed. The reason is here,
ready for it.

**The Vigil is the sharpest case.** The gods of holy dread watched the dark of the cathedral lit until nothing was
left to tremble at; they have their dark back now, in the ruins, and they feed the one body on Earth whose
founders felt the ritual. Those few felt it for what it was because they had spent their lives learning the
weight of their own thoughts: in cloisters and lodges and mountain hermitages, in silence kept for years. The gods
steer the Vigil toward the ruins, through the Pilgrims, and starve the Listeners. So the only people who suspect
the calamity had an author are fed by its authors.

### What the player knows, and when

A player begins knowing none of the above. What they meet is a world that has stopped working and a set of
explanations that are all wrong. That the calamity has an author at all is a **discovery**, arrived at
through storylines over a long time, and it is the discovery the whole setting is arranged around. Behind it
stand two more: that the figure guiding the house is one of those authors, and that the house itself is the same
kind of thing. And past those, for a server event far ahead, where the gods came from.

**Rising in the Vigil is one road toward the first discovery.** Its first circle may say nothing of the weight;
each circle above says a little more, in the words of the wing that holds it, and none of them says it right.

**So nothing in the interface may state any of it.** Piety is piety as the age understands it - observance, standing
with the church, what a house owes and is owed - and never a wire to a god. A resource, a mechanic or a line
of dialogue that gives the game away early spends the only card the setting is holding. Where something must
gesture at it, it gestures the way the world would: as a coincidence, a bad omen, or a man who will not
explain how he knew.

## Two clocks

There are two times, and everything that happens carries both.

| | What it is | What runs on it |
|---|---|---|
| **World-time** | The story's clock: a date in Earth's calendar - uneven months, leap years and all. The world opened on the first of January, 1790. | The chronicle, and the map as it was at a given moment. |
| **Player-time** | The order in which things were actually done at a keyboard. Today, then tomorrow. | Arbitration between players, and the event store's own order. |

**The game is played chronologically.** The world moves forward and every house moves forward with it. A
house's history is written, not visited: what happened in 1793 happened once, while people were playing it,
and afterwards it is a thing to look back at rather than a place to return to.

The two clocks differ by a fixed amount: **a real day is a world month**, the same for everyone. A month a
visit is what lets a house's history move at the pace a person actually plays at. **There is no
fast-forward.** The world is shared, so its speed is not anybody's to change; a
player who could run time on would be running it on for everyone standing in it. See decision record 0053.

## A house

A house is a **bloodline**, not a roster. It has a handful of **key figures** the player acts through, and
uncounted unknown members behind them. There is **no losing**: if every key figure dies, someone rises to
the task. What a house has instead of a lose state is a **power state** — a house with no organisation and
one new key figure is in a low one, and one with a cathedral, a court and three key figures is in a high
one — and the power state rises and falls across the centuries.

## The house-spirit

What never falls is the **house-spirit**: what a house has made of itself through everything it has done,
expressed as traits, stats and special effects. A house that has been greedy and lazy and taken the path of
least resistance has those perks, and they colour the rolls it gets.

A house can suffer a takedown by the world and gain spirit bonuses that stay with it. That is how past
glories never fade: a low power state in one century is played with the spirit earned in all the others.

The spirit is mostly a **permanent progression**. Traits can fade by decision, if a player wants to recraft
their identity, and there may be short-lived bonuses. **Spirit items** are the example: guide the house down
an evil route and it earns a permanent sacrificial knife — a spirit item, not a physical one — that can be
applied to a decision to sacrifice a key figure at great cost. An item can be a spirit item or a physical
possession in the world; spiritness is an attribute of the item.

What the house-spirit is, under all of that, is the house itself: a withered energy holding on to a family, as
*The houses are energies too* says. Nothing in the interface may say so.

## Things that come to be, and cease to be

A cathedral, a person, a stretch of territory: each exists between the moment it comes to be and the
moment it stops, and what it is between those moments is shared by every house that can see it. One house
may raise a thing and another end it. In engineering terms each is its own stream with a lifetime in
world-time; see `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

A key figure is not one of these things. It is a person the house has taken in: the person is what comes to
be and ceases to be, and the house's tie to them begins and ends in the house's own history. See decision
record 0055.

## NPCs

NPC factions and famous characters have streams of their own: a decision AI plays them, and they work
roughly as human players do — but as a more constant force, some of them more the shape of the landscape
than a figure in it. An NPC house is a house; the difference is who issues its commands. Adam's word for one is
a **house bot**: when the world opens, every place in every faction is held by one, and players rise into those
places as they are made ready to.

## Not decided

- **Whether one person can serve two houses.** The engine lets a character be a key figure of several
  houses at once (decision 0055), and nothing in the fiction yet says what that would mean: a shared
  bishop, a double agent, a marriage that binds two lines, or a guide in several houses' corners at once.
- **What the chrono-engine is for**, now that it is no longer the thing that chooses the day.
- **Why the gods took fright**, beyond the fear `STORY.md` gives them, that reason would part people from the
  green world: what each of them feared in particular, which is the question every storyline about them
  eventually has to answer.
- **How many gods there are, and which ones** besides the three who broke the plan. Also whether the council
  learns it was betrayed, whether a house can side with the two who remain of the three, and whether that is a
  route or an ending.
- **What the ruins do to those who go in**, beyond what `STORY.md` hints - coming home
  wanting what they never wanted - and whether a ruin can ever be made clean.
- **Whether the apocalypse can be stopped**, survived, or only lived through. The house has no lose state;
  the world might.
- **Whether a house has a guide of its own or shares one** with the houses near its ruin, as *The guides*
  proposes, and how a guide chooses its next move.
- **Which temperament of guide comes first**, and how many there are.
- **What a house's energy was before it clung**: one of the council, a lesser thing, or something nobody
  remembers; whether a house can ever learn it, and what it does then.
- **What comes from the nebula** when Oriel's energy passes it, and what it wants.
- **How far into the enlightenment the world opens**, and how much of the interruption has already happened
  when a player founds their first seat.
- **The four factions besides the Vigil**: their names, how each is ordered, and which gods hold the Company and
  the courts in exile.
- **The Vigil's names**, its own and its wings', and whether three wings is the right number.
- **What the Listeners can learn**, and whether the Vigil can ever find out what feeds it.
- **How a house comes down from the top of a faction**, and what its spirit keeps: tabled on 2026-10-20, so that
  it never feels bad for the player who climbed.
