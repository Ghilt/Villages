# 0064. The edge of sight has surface tension

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-26
- Builds on 0061, which says what a house sees; changes what its vision and its revealed ground are shaped like

## Context

Decision 0061 made vision the circles of the points lasting today and revealed ground every circle there has
been. Drawn, that is a scatter of hard-edged circles with sharp notches where two overlap and slivers of haze
where two nearly touch. Adam asked for it smoothed, with a picture: the notches rounded off, the short gaps
between circles filled, the circles bleeding into each other "like surface tension", and "only over short
distances".

Three things constrain how. **Revealed ground piles up points**: a key figure moved a hundred times around one
valley leaves a hundred circles over it, so whatever smooths the edge must not grow where circles lie over
one another. **The server holds back by the same shape the page draws** (0061, and step 4 of the plan), so
the smoothed shape has to be answerable at a single place on the server, where there is no grid and ten
thousand houses. And **the page draws from a distance field** (0062), so it needs a distance to the smoothed
edge rather than a yes or no.

## Decision

**Vision and revealed ground are each closed by a ball as big as the surface tension.** A place is covered
when it is inside a circle, or when every ball of that size that would contain it would stray into a circle:
whatever the ball cannot get into is filled. That is a morphological closing, and it does exactly what the
picture asks and nothing else. A notch where two circles overlap is rounded off by a curve the size of the
ball; a gap narrow enough that the ball cannot pass is bridged, and one a little wider has each side reach
out towards the other; a gap the ball passes through is left open; a lone circle is left exactly as it is,
because the ball rolls all the way round it; and a place seen again and again changes nothing, because a
circle inside the covered ground has nothing to fill.

- **The tension is 40 km**, `Vision.SURFACE_TENSION` in the domain. Between two seat-sized circles it bridges
  gaps of up to about 56 km and leaves one of 80 km open. It is a matter of taste, picked from Adam's picture,
  where gaps of about one radius are bridged.
- **The server answers exactly**, as `Coverage` in `protocol`. A place is covered when the disc of every centre
  a containing ball could have lies inside the circles grown by the tension; a disc lies inside a set of discs
  when its rim does and when no two of them cross inside it at a point no third one covers, because every hole
  among discs has a corner like that. It is cheap where it is asked most - a place inside a circle or far from
  every circle is answered at once - and the rest only near an edge.
- **The page draws it from its distance field**: a texel is inside when no texel outside the grown circles lies
  within a ball's width, which one distance transform over the grid says for every texel together, and the
  distance to the edge falls out of the same numbers. It is within a texel of the exact answer, and the edge
  is drawn soft by more than that. Which marks stand in vision, and so go over the fog (0063), the page asks
  of `coverageOf`, the TypeScript half of `Coverage`.
- **Both ends are held to `contracts/fixtures/coverage.json`**, generated by trying ball centres by brute force,
  independently of both, and the page's field to the same rows to within the width of its soft edge.
- **The tension crosses the wire** in `yourSight`, rather than being a constant at both ends, so that the server
  stays the one place a sight is worked out and a change to the number is made once. Wire version 12.
- The circles are the map's circles at their own latitudes, as in 0061, grown by the tension at theirs, and
  the ball is as big as the tension at the place's own latitude. A closing wants one ball everywhere; within
  the few hundred kilometres a closing ever reaches across, the difference is a few per cent, and both ends
  make the same one.

## Consequences

What a house sees is bigger than its circles wherever they are close together, and **that ground is seen in
the full sense**: a house standing in a bridge between two circles is sent, and a person standing in a bridge
between two lasting circles is seen. The fog does not only look smoother; the tension is part of the rule.

A lone circle is still exactly 34 km, so nothing about one seat or one key figure changes.

The page's field costs more to work out: about 5 ms for a house with one seat, 15 ms with a few points, and a
tenth of a second for a trail of sixty, once for each change to the vision points and never while the camera
moves. That is about what a trail of sixty cost before this step, because the same step made the circles
themselves cheaper to lay down. Working the field out off the main thread is the answer when it matters, and
so is folding the ended points together, which 0061 already names.

## Alternatives considered

**A smooth minimum of the circles' distances**, the metaball blend. It rounds notches and bridges gaps with one
formula, and it swells wherever circles lie over one another: two circles on the same spot push the edge out
by a quarter of the blend's width, and a spot visited again and again pushes it out by nearly all of it. The
version that does not depend on order swells by the width times the logarithm of how many circles overlap.
Revealed ground is exactly the case of many circles over one another, so it would grow where a house has
looked hardest.

**The closing worked out on a grid at both ends.** One implementation, and agreement for free. It would keep a
grid for every house on the server, or build one for every question about a place, at ten thousand houses.

**A smooth outline drawn over the circles without changing the rule.** The haze would lie over ground the
server calls seen, and lift from ground it holds back, which is the disagreement 0061 exists to prevent.
