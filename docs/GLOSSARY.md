# Glossary

> **What belongs here:** every word the project uses for a thing, and what it means. **What does not:** the
> reasoning behind any of them, which is in the decision records, and anything that is not a word.

The shared vocabulary of Villages. These words are used verbatim in code, in event names and in the interface. If a
term changes here, it changes in all three places in the same commit. Names in `code font` are identifiers that exist
in the source.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Aggregate** | A consistency boundary with its own event stream. `House` and `Character`, each decided by its own `Decider`. |
| **Action** | One thing a **Choice roll** can offer: a **Rarity**, the **Pool**s it is in, and a way of dealing it, which fills in its details from the roll's **Omen**s and the world at that moment - *a visit from a stranger from the farthest house you know of* names a real house. Dealt, an action is an **Option** of the choice the roll gives; **taking an action** is the house's answer, recorded as an event, with the option's effect following from the same decision. See decision records 0034 and 0083, and `docs/plans/choice-roll.md`. `Action`; `TakeAction`, `PlayerTookAction` |
| **Age** | How old a **Character** is: the whole world years since the day they were born, plus any years they were aged out of the ordinary. Counted, never ticked - no event marks a birthday - so at a world month for every real day a character ages a year every twelve real days. Not the age the world is in, which is the **Enlightenment**. See decision record 0055. Worked out in the browser, `ageOn`, from `bornOn` and `agedYears`, never below nought. |
| **Apocalypse** | What the gods are doing to the **Enlightenment**, and the interruption the world is living through: the social order failing, cities that have stopped working without being sacked, and new factions risen into the gap. It has authors and a plan, and the plan is already going wrong. No player begins knowing any of that. See `SETTING.md`. Not yet in the source. |
| **Area** | A closed region of the world: a fence, drawn as a line with an optional shade inside, which knows the terrain census of what it encloses. **The technical term, and the component everything fenced on the map reuses** - what one *means* belongs to whatever draws it, so the same shape is a house's **Holding** today and a castle's **Influence zone** later. See decision records 0032 and 0066. `Area`, `Fence`; `enclose` then `fenceAround` make one from a drawn path, and `areaAround` makes one a reach out from a point, ragged if asked |
| **Arrival** | A player saying hello. A player with no house and no choice pending is given the founding choice on arrival; a player with either is given nothing. `Arrival`, and `FoundingChoices` writes the words |
| **Arrangement** | How the two **Bay**s of the **Bays** divide the **Stage** between them, as one value with three settings: a **Strip** and the rest, half each, or the rest and a strip. **A property of the fold and not of the panes in it** - a bay is one end of a stage two things are sharing, and what is standing in it has nothing to say about how much of it there is. That is what makes the three sizes mean the same thing on every pane, and what stops the **Compass** resizing anything. Both bays put away is not an arrangement, so it is not a state a player can reach. Named by the **Pane size control** on either bay. See decision record 0046. `Arrangement`, and `askedIn` says which size each bay is drawn at |
| **Banner** | A house's heraldic device: a field of one or two tinctures, a division, and a charge. The visible identity of a house, shown in the interface and as its sprite on the map. `Banner` |
| **Bay** | Where a **Pane** sits: a rectangle the layout hands out, in both of the **Stage**'s forms. More than one pane can share one, taking turns behind a **Tab bar**. In the **Spread** they are the **Floor** and four **Tile**s on it; in the **Bays** they are the two boxes the **Stage** splits into once it is too small to show everything at once, divided along the stage's longer dimension, so a portrait stage gives a top and a bottom. How much of the stage each of those two gets is the **Arrangement**, which belongs to the fold rather than to whichever pane is standing in it. Distinct from the **Debug panel**, which is Adam's and is not on the stage at all. See decision records 0040 and 0046. `bayLayout` is the arithmetic and `bays.ts` is the manager |
| **Bays** | The **Stage**'s form when there is no longer room for everything at once: two **Bay**s cut along the stage's longer side, so a portrait stage gives a top and a bottom and a landscape one gives a left and a right. What it is at or below 600 pixels on its shorter side; above 660 it is the **Spread**, and between the two it keeps whichever it had. A **Pane** in it has **six states** - three sizes, each upright and turned - and the player says which of the three **Arrangement**s the two bays are in. See decision records 0040, 0041 and 0046. `twoBays`, and `formFor` says which form the stage is in |
| **Border** | The nine-slice picture a **Container** is framed in: one whole painted box, cut four times, its corners drawn once and its edges repeated or stretched along the box, so one picture frames a box of any size. Adam's, in `world/ui/borders/`, with a file beside each naming its cuts; one that was not painted as a border is made into one from a picture of a frame by `scripts/nine-slice.ts`. Not the **Seam**, and not a **Frame**, which is a message on the wire. See decision record 0068. `BorderLayer` |
| **Card** | A **Container** small enough to be one of many - a **Key figure** in a grid, something that happened - with a picture at its head or down its side, a name and what it is under the name, drawn by a **Recipe**, `card.figure` or `card.event`. Pressed anywhere, when it can be pressed: it lifts under the pointer, as a thing that can be picked up does. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `Card` |
| **Carousel** | One thing at a time, with a step either side and a dot per thing beneath: the small form of a **Pane** whose full form is a list, so the list can be glanced through in a strip of screen while the map stays up beside it. The **Choice roll**'s small size, and its only use so far. See decision record 0044. `Carousel` in `Choosing.tsx`, and the library's own in `ui/carousel/`, which the choice roll takes up when it is redone |
| **Causation id** | The id of the message that directly caused an event: for an event from a command, the command's id, which the debug log carries because commands are not stored. On every event's envelope as the `causationid` extension, and on the `event` frame. See decision record 0035. `StoredEvent.causationId` |
| **Cell** | A square of the world used to partition live updates and map queries: a quadkey at any zoom from 0 to 10, so a cell's ancestor is its prefix. Derived from a place wherever it is needed, never carried on an event. See decision record 0026. `Cell` |
| **Charge** | The figure borne on a banner, such as a tower, a stag or a star. `Charge` |
| **Choice** | A prompt and two to four options put to a house at a moment. It can expire: a choice nobody answers stops being relevant once the world has moved on. Shown to the player as a parchment. See decision record 0034. `Choice`, `GiveChoice`, `PlayerGivenChoice`, and `House.pending` is the one a house has |
| **Character** | A person in the world, and one day perhaps another kind of being, with a history of their own: a name, a **Sex**, a **Species**, the day they were born, four **Stat**s and a **Portrait**, and a place on the map when they have one. They can die. What a character is to a house is a tie the house keeps, never a kind of character: see **Key figure**. One stream each, addressed as `character:<uuid>`. See decision record 0055 and `MECHANICS.md`. `Character`, `CharacterDecider`, and `CharacterId.broughtIn` works out who a new one is |
| **Character dialogue** | The mechanic people talk to each other in: a **Dialogue** offered to the house and stepped through by the player, a line at a time by *continue* and a **Dialogue choice** where it branches, as speech bubbles from the left and the right by **Party** under a **Scene**. It covers a conversation, a monologue and a group discussion alike, it can pay the house as it goes, and it **Lapse**s when a hidden **Requirement** fails. What storylines are carried in. See `MECHANICS.md` and decision records 0071, 0069 and 0070. `DIALOGUE_PANE` is its pane, standing in the column down the right of the **Spread** |
| **Choice roll** | The mechanic a choice is offered by: the player presses **Take action** and pays for it, the **Fate engine** reads the **Omen**s, rolls a **Rarity** and deals a hand of three **Action**s of it, and the player must take one, though they can set the hand aside first. The world can one day raise one unprompted inside an event or a storyline. See `MECHANICS.md`. A **Pane** that is always in the bottom middle: the button when idle, a report card of omens and then the hand when rolled, the options laid out, the **Carousel**, or set aside to a strip, and the one pane whose size is the player's rather than the layout's. See decision records 0044 and 0083. `Roll`, `PlayerRolled` |
| **Command** | A request to change the world, named as an imperative: `FoundHouse`. It may be rejected. Commands are not stored. |
| **Compass** | A compass rose - four cardinal points filled and four between them drawn, inside a ring: the way to everything that is not on the screen. On a phone it is a disc standing in the **Stage**'s bottom right corner, the one thing that lies over a **Bay** rather than sitting in one, and one tap puts **Navigation** in the bay under the thumb - the bottom one on a tall phone, the right one on a wide one; pressing it again gives that bay back. It **never resizes anything**, and what is under it steps clear rather than being covered. It rode the seam between the bays until 0047, which is the one place on the stage that moves. In the **Spread** it is a tab in the corner instead. It replaced a kebab that opened a menu over the world, because a menu that covers what it is about has to be dismissed to be used. See decision records 0043 and 0047. `Compass`, and `NEAR_BAY` is which bay it opens |
| **Component library** | Every part the interface is built from - containers, controls, readouts, collections - in `apps/web/src/ui/`, importing nothing from the game, and shown in every variant and state on its own page, `/admin/inspect-ui-components`. A component is seen there before it is used. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md` and decision record 0068. `src/ui/` |
| **Container** | Any boxed thing in the interface - a **Pane**, a card, a dialog, a tooltip - as four layers: a **Border**, a **Fill**, none or several **Embellishment**s, and the content. Named by a **Recipe**. A container in a container is two containers. See decision record 0068. `Container` |
| **Copy** | Land of an edited master that came from the same place on Earth as a larger piece did: a paste, or the Clone Stamp. **Invented land** that looks like somewhere, because every real place lands in the world at most once. Orange striped under the Origin renderer. See the **Origin map**. `ORIGIN_CLONE`, `markClones` |
| **Correlation id** | The id of the story an event belongs to. The first message of a story is its own correlation, and everything that follows from it carries the same; the server decides which story a command continues. On every event's envelope as the `correlationid` extension, and the debug panel tags each event line with it. See decision record 0035. `StoredEvent.correlationId` |
| **Decider** | The pure pair of functions that make up an aggregate: decide turns a command and the current state into events, evolve folds an event into the state. It holds the rules of the game and depends on nothing. One shape for every aggregate, so the loop that reads, folds, decides and appends is written once. `Decider`, and `HouseDecider` is the house's |
| **Delta** | An incremental change pushed to a client after its snapshot: a house appeared, or a house changed. |
| **Debug panel** | Adam's space down the right-hand side of the window: everything that helps while the game is being built, in one docked column, and nothing a player will ever have. It takes room from the **Stage** rather than floating over it, so narrowing it restricts the play area as a smaller screen would; a toggle in its top corner confines the stage to a phone. It minimises to a badge and remembers that. Which world is drawn and what draws it are part of it. **Only in a development build**: a production build leaves it out whole, and the stage has the window. See decision records 0023, 0039 and 0079. `DebugPanel`, brought in by `debug/devTools.ts`, and `stageBox.ts` is the arithmetic |
| **Desk** | The surface the map lies on: seen once the camera is pulled back past the fit zoom, where the sheet stops covering the window, and hidden above it. It has edges, because something can see past them - pull back past the floor and it stands in a **Room**. A drawn grain for now; later the player's workbench, with the house's papers on it. See decision records 0033 and experiment 0009. `Desk`, `deskOnScreen`, and `floorZoom` says how far back the camera may go |
| **Dialogue** | One conversation offered to a house, recorded whole when it is offered: a title, a **Scene**, its speakers, each in a **Party**, every **Line** and every **Dialogue choice**, and its **Requirement**s, so the roads not taken are history too, as a **Choice**'s are. A house can have several waiting at once, and keeps every one it has finished. What the player meets it as is **Character dialogue**. See decision record 0071. `Dialogue`, offered by `OfferDialogue` as `PlayerOfferedDialogue`, and `House.Founded.openDialogues` is what a house has open; `PlayerCompletedDialogue` is one reaching its end |
| **Dialogue choice** | The point in a **Dialogue** where the player picks what is said or done next instead of pressing *continue*: two to four options, each a **Line**, written when the dialogue is offered. **Not a Choice roll**, though the two are alike: some are plain choices of what to say, some will one day carry a roll's gamble, and what one offers can be shaped by the world. Taking an option is a **Dialogue step**. See decision record 0071. `Then.DialogueChoice` |
| **Dialogue step** | What a house's history records of a **Dialogue** being read: an option of a **Dialogue choice** taken, or a **Line** reached that has an effect, is marked to be recorded, or ends the dialogue. The plain lines between are read in the browser and never written down, so a conversation is a handful of lines in the event page rather than one a press, and a dialogue reopened picks up at its last step. What a step does follows it as the world's own events, from the same decision. See decision record 0071. `CompleteDialogueStep`, sent from the page as `completeDialogueStep` and recorded as `PlayerCompletedDialogueStep`; `Dialogue.isStep` says which lines are steps, and `Dialogue.walk` rebuilds the rest from them |
| **Diamond** | One cell of the grid the ground's colour is read on, at its finest: a square of world space one world unit across, turned as far as the ground's grid is turned (26 degrees for a player), which the tipped camera draws as a diamond. As wide as a **Master pixel**; away from the equator Mercator draws a master pixel taller than it is wide, so at Mordaunt's latitude one is about one and a half diamonds tall. What an **Area** is fenced on and what its **Terrain census** counts, whatever size the ground's cells are painted at. See decision records 0060 and 0066. `DiamondGrid`, `diamondAt`, and `lattice.ts` holds the arithmetic the painter and the fence share |
| **Dialog** | A question that has to be answered before anything else - kill this **Key figure**? - over the whole page, the page dimmed behind it and out of reach until it is answered or put away. What cannot be undone in one is answered by **Hold to confirm**. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `Dialog` |
| **Dive** | How the loop turns over, and it turns over inside the mark for your own house. The camera is pinned at the closest zoom over the seat and the whole scene is scaled up about the middle of the castle until its pixels are the size of the window; the **Room** is inside them, growing out as you go in and shrinking back as you come out. By the time it is the window it is the same room at the same size the real one is about to be, so the camera can be moved underneath and not be seen doing it. It runs on a rail rather than on the wheel, because a passage is a thing that finishes. Only over your own holding, and only with a house to have one. See experiment 0009. `Dive`, `attachPull`, and `dive.ts` is the arithmetic |
| **Divider** | A painted rule between two things, as the moodboard draws them between its sections: a line laid between its two ends at whatever width it is given, with a **Knot** in its middle or a section's name on it. Adam's, in `world/ui/dividers/`, cut only at its two ends. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md` and `AUTHORING-THE-ART.md`. `Divider`, `Rule` |
| **Division** | How the field of a banner is partitioned: plain, per pale, per fess, quarterly. `Division` |
| **Domain** | A house's reach over land and what it yields: a resource that **gates** rather than being spent. A house needs enough of it to attempt a thing, and attempting the thing does not use it up. **Domain is the word the player sees; the code says `NATURAL_DOMAIN`**, because `domain` already names the innermost layer of the server and one word for two things is a word nobody can grep for. `Resource.NATURAL_DOMAIN` |
| **Effect** | What taking an option does, carried on the option itself so that a choice is self-describing, and what a **Line** of a **Dialogue** does. Three kinds so far: one founds the house at a seat with a name and a banner, one grants the house some of a resource, and one spends some of it, which is refused when the house has not that much free. The decider turns the taken option's effect, or the line's, into events in the same decision as the action or the step. `Effect`, with `Effect.FoundAt`, `Effect.Grant` and `Effect.Spend` |
| **Embellishment** | A picture pinned to a **Container**'s corner or the middle of one of its edges - a cherub, a drape, a cross - which is not part of the **Border**. It says its **Overhang**, and takes none of the content's room: it stands where it is pinned, over the border or out past it. Adam's, in `world/ui/embellishments/`. Not the **Ornament**, which is drawn in code and is being replaced by these. See decision record 0068. `Embellishment` |
| **Enlightenment** | The age the world is in: reason spreading through instruments, presses, academies and letters, growing up through what came before rather than replacing it - castles still stand and a cathedral is still the largest thing for a hundred miles. Interrupted by the **Apocalypse**. The world opened on the first of January, 1790, in Earth's own years. See `SETTING.md`. Not yet in the source. |
| **Event** | Something that happened, named in the past tense: `HouseFounded`. Events are the only durable state and are never edited or deleted. Every one is either the world's or the player's; see the two rows below. Where one is kept is a separate question, answered by its stream: a house's are `HouseEvent`s. `Event` |
| **Event of the player** | Something a player did, by which the world was reached into: being asked something, and answering it. The mechanics of play rather than a happening in the world, recorded all the same because they are how everything in the world came to happen. `villages.player.<verb>.v<n>`, and `PlayerEvent` in the code. A small set on purpose. See decision record 0034. |
| **Event of the world** | Something that happened in the world: a thing came to be, moved, changed or ended. What the chronicle is made of and what the map is drawn from. `villages.house.<verb>.v<n>`, and `WorldEvent` in the code. The half that grows. See decision record 0034. |
| **Event family** | The events of one mechanic on a house's stream: what it has, what it is asked, what it is told, whom it holds, what it sees. The third thing said of an event, after where it is kept and which half it is in; the founding is in none, because every mechanic reads it. A fold with nothing to say about a mechanic passes its family in one branch. See decision record 0075. `ResourceEvent`, `ChoiceEvent`, `DialogueEvent`, `KeyFigureEvent`, `VisionEvent` |
| **Event id** | The identity of one event: the CloudEvent `id`, a UUID minted when it is written. What a consumer deduplicates by. See decision records 0024 and 0035. |
| **Event store** | The append-only record of every event, one stream per aggregate. MongoDB, through Occurrent. |
| **Eye** | The player's switch for keeping their own holdings in sight: with it open, their seat is drawn however far back the camera goes and the ground about it below the zoom where anyone else's starts. Theirs and nobody else's, so the world still thins out with distance. Costs nothing on the wire, because a player's own house already reaches them wherever they are looking. The first place in the bar along the bottom of the house card. Distinct from **Seeing everything**. See decision record 0038. `eyeStore`, and `always` on the sprite |
| **Face** | The picture a button is drawn on, in `world/ui/controls/`: stretched between its end caps to fit its words when it has a file of cuts beside it, as a drape or an oval does, and laid whole when not, as a round one is. A round face may have its mark painted in or be plain for a drawn one. Its hovered, pressed and disabled states are light and shade over it unless they are painted as pictures of their own. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md` and `AUTHORING-THE-ART.md`. `faceOf` |
| **Fate engine** | The part of the game that rolls what can happen to a house at a moment: it works out the **Omen**s, rolls a **Rarity** from fixed odds, opens the **Pool**s the omens point at and deals a hand of **Action**s from them. The engine behind a **Choice roll**, and architecture rather than a mechanic: see `ARCHITECTURE.md` and decision record 0083. Its luck is the application's, never the domain's. Called the chrono-engine until 2026-09-19, when the chrono jump it was named for was dropped. |
| **Fault** | Something acting on its own that threw: a subscriber to the feed on an event, a listener at its work, or something scheduled for later. The feed says so and goes on without handing it that event again, so the read model is missing it and the fault is how anyone finds out: the server's log gets the stack, and every window's debug panel the one line, as an `error` frame with the code `fault`. See decision record 0029. `Fault`, reported to and watched on the `Faults` port |
| **Fence** | The boundary of an area: closed rings on the lattice between **Diamonds**, so every edge runs along the ground's own grid and a diamond is inside or outside and never both. Two areas drawn flush share their boundary exactly. Traced from the diamonds a drawn path took, by the same walk a coastline is traced with, and it carries the grid it was drawn on. See decision records 0032 and 0066. `Fence` |
| **Fill** | What is inside a **Container**'s **Border**: a picture that repeats without a seam, parchment, dark wood, velvet, or nothing. Adam's, in `world/ui/fills/`. See decision record 0068. `FillLayer` |
| **Floor** | The **Bay** that is the whole **Stage**, with every **Tile** lying on it: the map, in the **Spread**. The one bay exempt from the rule that bays never overlap, because everything overlaps it on purpose - which is what makes the map the ground the interface stands on rather than a pane competing with the rest for room. The **Bays** have no floor: there the map is a pane like any other. See decision record 0041. `bays.floor` in `bayLayout` |
| **Follower** | Something that folds the event feed from its beginning: a read model, or a listener that acts on what it hears, such as the pledge release. It subscribes when it is started rather than when it is made, and forgets everything it folded when the development world is wiped. See `ARCHITECTURE.md`, "What acts on its own", and decision records 0029 and 0036. `Follower`, `Resettable` |
| **Fog of war** | The mechanic of what a house knows about the world by where it can see: the map at three levels, **Vision**, **Revealed** and **Unknown**. It limits what a player is told about a place and nothing else: it stops no command and hides no terrain. What gives a house vision is a **Vision point**. The switch is Adam's, in the Debug map section, *fog of war: on* or *off*; off lifts all of it for that window. See `MECHANICS.md` and decision record 0061. |
| **Frame** | One message on the wire between the gateway and the browser: JSON text with a `type` field naming it. The fixtures in `contracts/fixtures/frames` own the format, and both ends are tested against them. `ClientFrame`, `ServerFrame` |
| **Free** | How much of a resource a house may actually do something with: what it has now, less what its live **Pledge**s hold. Every rule that asks whether a house can afford or attempt a thing asks this, which is what stops pledged reason being spent out from under the pledge. `House.Founded.freeOf` |
| **Gate** | A rule that asks whether a house has enough of a resource **Free**, and takes none of it: what **Domain** and **Influence** are for. Asking costs nothing, which is the whole difference between a gate and a price. Nothing gates anything yet, because there is nothing yet to build. `House.Founded.has` |
| **Gateway** | The service that holds player connections, serves snapshots and pushes deltas. Stateless apart from its live connections. |
| **Gazetteer** | The hand-kept list of named seats the founding choice draws from, in the server, until the server knows terrain. Every seat in it has been read out of the zoom 7 tiles of every master and is on land in all of them. `Gazetteer` |
| **Gods** | Aspects of what people are, wearing whatever shape an age hands them; the Greek gods were one such shape, a set of masks worn and set down, and not the thing itself. They took fright at the **Enlightenment** and authored the **Apocalypse**, and at least one of them broke the plan from inside, so they are a divided party improvising rather than a single will. **Nothing in the interface may say so**: that the calamity has an author at all is the discovery the setting is arranged around. See `SETTING.md`. Not yet in the source. |
| **Grid** | Things of one kind in rows and columns, every cell the same width; told how many rows as well, one that **never scrolls**, its cells sized to fit the room it is given and the same size however many things there are - the **Key figure**s' three by three. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `Grid` |
| **Ground form** | What a thing on the map *is*, drawn at a height in world units so that it grows with the terrain and holds its size against the ground at every zoom. A house's is a castle, four units tall, which is sixty-four pixels at the closest the camera comes. Its height is a map convention rather than a measurement: a surveyed castle would be a tenth of a unit and never seen. The other form is the **Icon**. See decision record 0051. `TwoForms.groundHeight`, and `pixelsPerWorldUnit` turns it into pixels |
| **Ground map** | What a master's ground is, cut by `world:ground` into a coarse grid over the world's square for the server to read: each cell's terrain class, whether a river runs through it, whether it is coast, and its biome. How the server knows what a player is looking at without taking a page's word for it. Output, per worktree and gitignored. See decision record 0084. `Ground` |
| **Hold to confirm** | How the interface asks for what cannot be undone - killing a key figure: a button held down for a second and a fifth, a fuse burning along under its words, and nothing done for a press let go sooner. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `HoldButton`, `holdOf` |
| **Holding** | What a house has on the map rather than in its name: today its seat and the ground about it, and later the buildings and works it raises. What the **Eye** keeps in sight. The ground is an **Area** about the seat: a ragged patch of diamonds, one piece with no holes, seeded by the house so it is the same shape on every screen, and worked out when asked for rather than stored. Its size and its raggedness are placeholders. `HoldingOverlay` draws it |
| **House** | The thing a player plays: a noble lineage with a name, a banner, a seat and a treasury. One event stream each, and one house a player - its identity is worked out from the player's, so founding twice is refused. `House` |
| **House status** | The mechanic that says how your own house stands: who it is, what it has and what it can do, always on screen and never opened. Carries the bar of the house's own levers, of which the **Eye** is the first. Distinct from an **Inspect window**, which is opened on a thing and can be about anybody's house. See `MECHANICS.md`. `HouseStatus`, and `HouseBar` is the bar. |
| **House-spirit** | What a house has made of itself through everything it has done, as traits and effects that colour its rolls. Mostly a permanent progression: a house taken down by the world keeps what its spirit earned. Not yet in the source. |
| **Icon** | What a thing on the map *means*, drawn at a height in screen pixels so that it stays the same size whatever the camera does. A house's is the same castle said in three strokes, twenty-two pixels tall, because the detailed one is unreadable that small. It trades places with the **Ground form** by crossfading where the two are the same size. See decision record 0051. `TwoForms.iconHeight` |
| **Influence** | A house's reach over people and politics: a resource that **gates**, exactly as **Domain** does. Distinct from an **Influence zone**, which is an **Area** drawn on the map and not a resource. `Resource.INFLUENCE` |
| **Influence zone** | A use an **Area** can be put to: a region something on the map has reach over, dressed as influence. Not a technical term and not a shape of its own - calling an area a zone says what it is for and nothing about what it is. Nothing has one yet; the ground about a seat is a **Holding**. Not yet in the source. |
| **Inspect window** | The mechanic that says what one thing in the world is: a window on a **Subject**, with what the world knows about it and the **Links** worth following on from it. Not the map's - a **Pane** anything can open, and the map is only the first place a subject is pointed at. See `MECHANICS.md`. Exact only north-up. See decision record 0060. `flatWindow`, `drawnAt`, `flatPoint` |
| **Shows** | What a **Line** shows as it is reached while playing: a place, which the camera is taken to, or a house or a character, which an **Inspect window** is opened on - in the **Spread** the map moves beside the conversation and the window joins the rail, and on a phone the conversation goes to half and the other **Bay** shows it. Written with the line and sent only with a line still ahead, so nothing shows before its line; the page's to act on, and never recorded. A line read back shows nothing, Adam's call. See decision record 0071. `Shows`, on `Line.shows` |
| **Sight** | What a house can see: every **Vision point** it has had, oldest first, and what they add up to - its **Vision**, what it has **Revealed**, and the **Unknown** rest. Worked out by a read model from where the house's seat and its people stand, never stored, and sent to the player wherever they are looking. See decision record 0061. `Sight`, `SightProjection`, and `yourSight` on the wire |
| **Significance** | How much a thing matters to the world, from nothing to a hundred, carried on the event that made it and deciding how far out on the map it stays drawn. A hundred is drawn from the whole world. `Significance`, and `coarsestZoom` turns it into a zoom |
| **Slice** | One mechanic's part of the page's **Session** state: what it starts as, how each frame it cares about changes it, and what an end of the session does to it. Pure, so nothing in one sends, waits or says anything. See decision record 0078. `Slice`, in `slices.ts` |
| **Snapshot** | The current contents of a cell, sent when a client subscribes to it, before any deltas. |
| **Surface tension** | What makes the edge of a house's sight smooth: vision and **Revealed** ground are each their **Vision point**s' circles, and every gap or notch between them that a ball this big cannot get into. Close circles are seen across, a notch where two overlap is rounded off, a lone circle stays exactly its circle, and a spot seen again and again does not swell. Part of the rule, not only the look: what it joins is seen. 40 km today. Adam's words and picture; see decision record 0064. `Vision.SURFACE_TENSION`; `Coverage` and `coverageOf` answer it; `tension` on `yourSight` |
| **Span** | A stretch of world-time. A **Pledge** is asked for a span of months; a **Character** on the map stands in one place for a span, from the day they were moved there until the day they were moved on, taken off the map or died, and a map at a moment is the spans that hold that day. See decision record 0055. `CharacterSpan` |
| **Species** | What kind of being a **Character** is: a person, and only a person so far. A pet would be a character of another species rather than a different sort of thing. Not called a kind, because a **Kind** is a sort of scenery. `Species` |
| **Spread** | The **Stage**'s form when there is room for everything at once: the map filling it, the **House status** in a corner, and any **Inspect window** in a rail beside them. What it is above 600 pixels on its shorter side; below that it is the **Bays**. See decision record 0040. `FOLDS_UNDER`, and `bayLayout` says which form the stage is in |
| **Stage** | The box the player's interface is laid out in: the map, the **Desk**, the overlays, the **House status**, any **Inspect window** and the **Parchment**, and nothing of Adam's. It is the window minus the **Debug panel**, and it is resized by two grips or confined to a phone, which is how a mechanic is checked at a shape there is no monitor for. It has two forms, the **Spread** and the **Bays**. See decision records 0039 and 0040. `.stage`, and `stageBox` says how big it is |
| **Stat** | One of a character's four qualities - **Piety**, **Reason**, **Kindness** and **Physical** - each a whole number from 1 to 20, rolled with three six-sided dice when a key figure is added. A key figure's power is their stats, shown as a mark, a word and a number. The house-spirit's "stats" in `SETTING.md`, and the house's stats the **Fate engine** is said to read, are older uses of the word and not yet these. `Stat`, a type of its own apart from `Resource`; drawn by `Mark`, whose piety and reason are the resources' own marks |
| **Stock** | How much of one resource a house has: a total, a rate per second, and the instant that was true. What is pledged against it is **not** kept here - it is the sum of the house's live pledges, because one fact in two places is one place too many. See decision record 0006. `Stock`, and `emptyStocks` makes the five a house is founded with |
| **Stream** | The ordered events of one aggregate instance, addressed as `house:<uuid>` or `character:<uuid>`. Where an event is kept, which is a separate question from what kind of thing it is. |
| **Strip** | A **Pane** put away: a band 44 pixels across with the pane's name on it and the **Pane size control** beside it, and unless the pane says otherwise that is the whole of it. The same band turned a quarter in landscape, 44 pixels wide down the side of the stage. One shape and one thickness for every pane, so that putting anything away looks like putting anything else away. Two panes say otherwise: the map's strip carries no control and is itself the way back, and **Navigation**'s carries the marks of what it could put in the other bay, because that is the strip a player arrives in by pressing the **Compass**. An **Inspect window**'s strip is its first row - its mark, the thing's name, its control and, on a desktop, its close - so it says which one rather than what kind. What **Minimise** produces. See decision records 0046 and 0047. `STRIP` is how many pixels |
| **Subject** | The one thing an **Inspect window** is about: a house, an area or a **Character** today, and later anything the world grows that can be pointed at. A tagged identity rather than a bare id, so that one window can render every kind of thing the way one **Parchment** renders every **Choice**. One store for both kinds, replacing the two selections that used to run side by side. `Subject`, and `subjects.ts` holds what is open |
| **The pull** | How far the eye has drawn back past the map's floor, from nothing at the floor to one in the **Room**. Not a zoom: the camera is pinned and MapLibre does no work at all while it moves, and what changes is a scale on what is already drawn. It shrinks the **Desk** away and opens the room around it, and spent, it hands over to the **Dive**. See experiment 0009. `pullStore`, `attachPull`, and `roomBox.ts` is the arithmetic |
| **Take action** | The button that provokes a **Choice roll**, in words rather than a mark, Adam's call: greyed out, saying why, while a choice is pending or the price is not free. Not **taking an action**, which is answering the hand it deals. |
| **Tab bar** | Several **Pane**s sharing one **Bay** and taking turns in it, with a strip of marks across its top saying which is up. The panes declare that they share by naming the same bay; the layout never decides it, so a tab cannot appear and vanish as the window is dragged. Whether panes sharing a bay take turns or **stack** is the bay's own property - the rail stacks its windows and never grows tabs. The tile is **one size whichever pane is up**, and its one **Pane size control** stands at the far end of the bar, so pressing a tab never resizes anything. Marks and no words, because a column is 26rem at its widest. Only in the **Spread**. See decision records 0042 and 0057 and `LOOK-AND-FEEL.md`. `TabBar`, and `showingIn` says which is up. The **Component library** has its own `TabBar`, in `src/ui/tabs/`, which the game's will be drawn with when the stage is redone |
| **Terrain census** | How many **Diamonds** of each terrain class an area encloses, each read at its middle out of the zoom 7 tiles, as the painter reads its colour. Derived from the fence whenever it is asked for rather than stored, so a repainted world or a moved edge means a fresh answer. `Census`, and `censusOf` takes one. |
| **Tile** | A **Bay** of the **Spread** that is not the **Floor**: the column's rail and corner, the one along the bottom middle, and the column down the right edge, which is cut last from what the others leave (decision record 0069). **Tiles never overlap each other and none leaves the stage**, which is a test over every size the spread can be rather than a claim - it is why the layout is arithmetic and not a stylesheet. An empty tile takes no room at all. The corner is the column's width, except opened out on a pane that asks to reach along the bottom, which the rail never does; see decision record 0056. See decision record 0041. `TILES`, and `bayLayout` says where each one is |
| **Tincture** | A heraldic colour. Metals are or and argent; colours are gules, azure, sable, vert and purpure. A charge of a metal goes on a field of a colour, and the reverse. `Tincture` |
| **Title plate** | The cartouche a place's name and its figure sit on, as the moodboard's Paris has it, and at a little over half the size a pane's name: a **Container** whose **Border** is the plate, as wide as its words. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `TitlePlate`, `plate.place`, `plate.heading` |
| **Toast** | Something that happened, said in a corner of the screen and gone once it has been read, longer for more words and never while it is being looked at. Gone for good: what it said is the event log's to keep. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `Toasts` |
| **Trail** | What the player has looked at, newest first, twelve steps back: the whole content of **Navigation**'s Recent section. Distinct from what is *open*, which is three at most and forgotten as a window closes. **The only thing on the stage that survives a reload**, because a trail that empties itself is not a trail. A step keeps the name the thing was looked at under, since the trail outlives what it points at. See decision record 0042. `trail.ts`, and the window is what records a name |
| **Treasury** | What a house has: a **Stock** of each of the five resources, and what of them is **Pledge**d. The **Part** of a founded house that decides its resource commands and folds its resource events. Until the five resources arrived it was the name of the one the world had, which is **Wealth** now. See decision record 0075. `Treasury` |
| **Unknown** | Ground a house has never seen. Only the terrain reaches it there, and anything of significance 80 or more, which is what a really big event means. Drawn as a dark haze that the contours of the map show through. One of the three levels of **Fog of war**. See decision record 0061. |
| **Vision** | Where a house can see right now: the circles of the **Vision point**s lasting today, joined by the **Surface tension**. Everything there is shown, and nothing is drawn over it. One of the three levels of **Fog of war**. Not `VISION.md`, which is what the whole experience is for. See decision record 0061. |
| **Vision point** | Somewhere a house sees from: a place, how far it sees around it in kilometres on the ground, and the **Span** of world-time it gives vision over. Anything can give one. Today a house's seat gives one from its founding, each of its key figures standing on the map gives one for as long as they stand in one place, and a vision given outright gives one until it is taken away; all of them see 34 km, a circle about the size of Mallorca. An ended point leaves what it covered **Revealed**. See decision record 0061. `VisionPoint`, `VisionSource`, `Vision.MALLORCA`; given outright, `GiveVision`, `HouseGainedVision` and `HouseLostVision`; whether a place is within one, `Reach` |
| **Warp** | The pure transformation that turns a place on Earth into a place in our world: control points that drag their surroundings, a seeded wobble, and optionally a turn of the globe. Applied once, at build time, and configured by `world/warp.json`. |
| **Wipe** | Emptying the development world: every stream and every projection cleared, and every session told, so that each browser arrives anew. A debug panel button, and not the wipe of decision record 0011. See decision record 0036. |
| **World** | The single shared place every player is on: a warped Earth, generated by us and canonical. Its coordinates are longitude and latitude, and they are not Earth's. There are no shards. |
| **Wealth** | Gold and treasure: the resource that is **spent**, so spending lowers it. The only one of the five that goes down by being used. Replaced the **Treasury**, which was the only resource the world used to have. `Resource.WEALTH` |
| **World-time** | The story's clock: a day in the world's calendar, counted from the first day of year one. One world month passes for every real day, the same for everyone, and there is no fast-forward. What the chronicle and the map at a moment run on. It moves forward only; the game is played chronologically. Distinct from player-time. See decision record 0053. `WorldTime`; on every event as `worldTime`, and read as a date in Earth's own calendar by `Calendar` |
