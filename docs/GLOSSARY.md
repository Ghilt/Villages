# Glossary

> **What belongs here:** every word the project uses for a thing, and what it means. **What does not:** the
> reasoning behind any of them, which is in the decision records, and anything that is not a word.

The shared vocabulary of Villages. These words are used verbatim in code, in event names and in the interface. If a
term changes here, it changes in all three places in the same commit. Names in `code font` are identifiers that exist
in the source.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Aggregate** | A consistency boundary with its own event stream. `House`, `Character` and `Structure`, each decided by its own `Decider`, and the world's own record of what it runs on, `WorldRecord`, decided by `WorldDecider` on the one stream `world` (0097). |
| **Action** | One thing a **Choice roll** can offer: a **Rarity**, the **Pool**s it is in, and a way of dealing it, which fills in its details from the roll's **Omen**s and the world at that moment - *a visit from a stranger from the farthest house you know of* names a real house. Dealt, an action is an **Option** of the choice the roll gives; **taking an action** is the house's answer, recorded as an event, with the option's effect following from the same decision. See decision records 0034 and 0083, and `docs/plans/choice-roll.md`. `Action`; `TakeAction`, `PlayerTookAction` |
| **Age** | How old a **Character** is: the whole world years since the day they were born, plus any years they were aged out of the ordinary. Counted, never ticked - no event marks a birthday - so at a world month for every real day a character ages a year every twelve real days. Not the age the world is in, which is the **Enlightenment**. See decision record 0055. Worked out in the browser, `ageOn`, from `bornOn` and `agedYears`, never below nought. |
| **Apocalypse** | What the gods are doing to the **Enlightenment**, and the interruption the world is living through: the social order failing, the great cities fallen to ruins that people shun, and new factions risen into the gap. It has authors and a plan, the **Ritual** of 1785, and the plan went wrong from inside and is going wrong still. No player begins knowing any of that. See `SETTING.md` and `STORY.md`. Not yet in the source. |
| **Area** | A closed region of the world: a fence, drawn as a line with an optional shade inside, which knows the terrain census of what it encloses. **The technical term, and the component everything fenced on the map reuses** - what one *means* belongs to whatever draws it, so the same shape is a house's **Holding** today and a castle's **Influence zone** later. See decision records 0032 and 0066. `Area`, `Fence`; `enclose` then `fenceAround` make one from a drawn path, and `areaAround` makes one a reach out from a point, ragged if asked |
| **Arrival** | A player saying hello. A player with no house and no choice pending is given the founding choice on arrival; a player with either is given nothing. `Arrival`, and `FoundingChoices` writes the words |
| **Arrangement** | How the two **Bay**s of the **Bays** divide the **Stage** between them, as one value with three settings: a **Strip** and the rest, half each, or the rest and a strip. **A property of the fold and not of the panes in it** - a bay is one end of a stage two things are sharing, and what is standing in it has nothing to say about how much of it there is. That is what makes the three sizes mean the same thing on every pane, and what stops the **Compass** resizing anything. Both bays put away is not an arrangement, so it is not a state a player can reach. Named by the **Pane size control** on either bay. See decision record 0046. `Arrangement`, and `askedIn` says which size each bay is drawn at |
| **Balance** | The **Catalogue** of the numbers that belong to no one entry of another: what a **Choice roll** costs and deals - its price, its hand, the odds of each **Rarity** and what an **Action** of each grants - when an **Omen** is read, how small an island is, how old a newcomer can be. `world/catalogues/balance.json`, each group of it with a `$comment` saying what its numbers do, shown and edited at `/admin/inspect-balance`. Every number is read as something is decided and what it decided is written down, so a retune never moves the past. See decision record 0097. `Balance`, `BalanceFile`, and `parseBalance` in the world package |
| **Banner** | A house's heraldic device: a field of one or two tinctures, a division, and a charge. The visible identity of a house, shown in the interface and as its sprite on the map. `Banner` |
| **Bay** | Where a **Pane** sits: a rectangle the layout hands out, in both of the **Stage**'s forms. More than one pane can share one, taking turns behind a **Tab bar**. In the **Spread** they are the **Floor** and four **Tile**s on it; in the **Bays** they are the two boxes the **Stage** splits into once it is too small to show everything at once, divided along the stage's longer dimension, so a portrait stage gives a top and a bottom. How much of the stage each of those two gets is the **Arrangement**, which belongs to the fold rather than to whichever pane is standing in it. Distinct from the **Debug panel**, which is Adam's and is not on the stage at all. See decision records 0040 and 0046. `bayLayout` is the arithmetic and `bays.ts` is the manager |
| **Bays** | The **Stage**'s form when there is no longer room for everything at once: two **Bay**s cut along the stage's longer side, so a portrait stage gives a top and a bottom and a landscape one gives a left and a right. What it is at or below 600 pixels on its shorter side; above 660 it is the **Spread**, and between the two it keeps whichever it had. A **Pane** in it has **six states** - three sizes, each upright and turned - and the player says which of the three **Arrangement**s the two bays are in. See decision records 0040, 0041 and 0046. `twoBays`, and `formFor` says which form the stage is in |
| **Border** | The nine-slice picture a **Container** is framed in: one whole painted box, cut four times, its corners drawn once and its edges repeated or stretched along the box, so one picture frames a box of any size. Adam's, in `world/ui/borders/`, with a file beside each naming its cuts; one that was not painted as a border is made into one from a picture of a frame by `scripts/nine-slice.ts`. Not the **Seam**, and not a **Frame**, which is a message on the wire. See decision record 0068. `BorderLayer` |
| **Bridge** | What carries every stored event from the **Event store** to the **Broker**: it follows the store durably, keeping its place in the world's database, and counts an event carried only once the broker has confirmed it, so an event written while the server or the broker was down is published when both are back. Only for a world kept in MongoDB. See decision record 0094. `Bridge`, riding `MongoWorld.carry`; its place is `checkpoints` |
| **Broker** | RabbitMQ, which carries every stored event from the **Bridge** to whoever follows it, through the one exchange `villages.events`, in a **Virtual host** of the worktree's own, under a routing key of the event's type and the cell it happened in. The read models and the events log follow it; the **Reaction**s follow the store itself, so the world acts on while the broker is away. See decision records 0003 and 0094. `Broker`, `CarriedWorld` |
| **Broker feed** | The event feed a read model follows when the **Broker** carries the world: the history read from the store, then every event through a queue of the follower's own, named after it, which it makes again and reads the history again after when the broker has been away. Not a feed of its own kind: the same feed, its live half taken from the broker. See decision record 0094. `BrokerLive`, `MongoWorld.follow` |
| **Card** | A **Container** small enough to be one of many - a **Key figure** in a grid, something that happened - with a picture at its head or down its side, a name and what it is under the name, drawn by a **Recipe**, `card.figure` or `card.event`. Pressed anywhere, when it can be pressed: it lifts under the pointer, as a thing that can be picked up does. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `Card` |
| **Carousel** | One thing at a time, with a step either side and a dot per thing beneath: the small form of a **Pane** whose full form is a list, so the list can be glanced through in a strip of screen while the map stays up beside it. The **Choice roll**'s small size on a phone, and its only use so far; the spread has no small size for it since 0085. See decision record 0044. `Carousel` in `Choosing.tsx`, and the library's own in `ui/carousel/`, which the choice roll takes up when it is redone |
| **Cast** | The **Critter**s a kind of ground has, by its terrain class: the woods' songbirds, in time the lakes' fish. Each member is a kind of critter, how it moves, how common it is there, and the scenery it comes out of. Ground with no cast has no critters. `CASTS` |
| **Catalogue** | A file of the game's content under `world/catalogues/`, or a folder of them: the types of **Structure**, the balance, the pools and actions of the **Fate engine**, the character names, the **Dialogue**s, the **Quest**s. An entry's own numbers live with the entry, and a number that belongs to no one entry lives in the balance. Changed by a commit, like code; the game server checks one before it takes it on, records each version it takes on as `catalogueChanged` on the world's own stream, and is the one that writes one when an inspect page saves. Every catalogue has its inspect page. Not the event catalogue, `docs/EVENTS.md`, which is always called that. See decision record 0097. `StructureCatalogue` is the first; the folder and the rest are not yet in the source |
| **Causation id** | The id of the message that directly caused an event: for an event from a command, the command's id, which the debug log carries because commands are not stored. On every event's envelope as the `causationid` extension, and on the `event` frame. See decision record 0035. `StoredEvent.causationId` |
| **Cell** | A square of the world used to partition live updates and map queries: a quadkey at any zoom from 0 to 10, so a cell's ancestor is its prefix. Derived from a place wherever it is needed, never carried on an event. See decision record 0026. `Cell` |
| **Charge** | The figure borne on a banner, such as a tower, a stag or a star. `Charge` |
| **Choice** | A prompt and two to four options put to a house at a moment. It can expire: a choice nobody answers stops being relevant once the world has moved on. Shown to the player as a parchment. See decision record 0034. `Choice`, `GiveChoice`, `PlayerGivenChoice`, and `House.pending` is the one a house has |
| **Character** | A person in the world, and one day perhaps another kind of being, with a history of their own: a name, a **Sex**, a **Species**, the day they were born, four **Stat**s and a **Portrait**, and a place on the map when they have one. They can die. What a character is to a house is a tie the house keeps, never a kind of character: see **Key figure**. One stream each, addressed as `character:<uuid>`. See decision record 0055 and `MECHANICS.md`. `Character`, `CharacterDecider`, and `CharacterId.broughtIn` works out who a new one is |
| **Character dialogue** | The mechanic people talk to each other in: a **Dialogue** offered to the house and stepped through by the player, a line at a time by *continue* and a **Dialogue choice** where it branches, as speech bubbles from the left and the right by **Party** under a **Scene**. It covers a conversation, a monologue and a group discussion alike, it can pay the house as it goes, and it **Lapse**s when a hidden **Requirement** fails. What storylines are carried in. See `MECHANICS.md` and decision records 0071, 0069 and 0070. `DIALOGUE_PANE` is its pane, standing in the column down the right of the **Spread** |
| **Chip** | The **Choice roll** put away in the **Spread**: Take action and what a roll costs, or the hand that is waiting, in the middle of the bottom row between the corner and the right column, which lay themselves out as though it were not there and always leave it its least. It carries no **Pane size control**, because a press on it opens the pane out. On a phone the choice roll is put away to a **Strip** like any pane. See decision record 0085. `CHIP_WANTS`, `CHIP_LEAST`, and `choice--chip` |
| **Checklist** | What an objective node of a **Quest** opens to: one to six **Objective**s, a row each, with what is asked, a box, and a button when the player is the one who meets it. Every row must be ticked, in any order, before the node is done. See decision record 0096. `ObjectiveNode.objectives`, at most `ObjectiveNode.MOST`; `QuestWalk.ticked` is what is ticked where the quest stands |
| **Choice roll** | The mechanic a choice is offered by: the player presses **Take action** and pays for it, the **Fate engine** reads the **Omen**s, rolls a **Rarity** and deals a hand of three **Action**s of it, and the player must take one, though they can set the hand aside first. The world can one day raise one unprompted inside an event or a storyline. See `MECHANICS.md`. A **Pane** that is always in the bottom middle: the button when idle, a report card of omens and then the hand when rolled, the options laid out, or set aside to a **Chip**; in the **Spread** it is put away or opened out, and opened out it lies over everything with the **Veil** under it, and on a phone it has the **Carousel** too. See decision records 0044, 0083 and 0085. `Roll`, `PlayerRolled` |
| **Clearing** | The clear patch the **Clouds** leave round each of the player's marks drawn far out: their cut is lifted there, whole over the mark and easing out beyond it, so they thin away from their own edges rather than having a circle cut out, and their shadows keep off the mark the same way. As many as thirty-two a frame, the nearest the middle. See decision record 0098. `clearingsFrom`, `iconsDrawn` |
| **Clouds** | Weather drawn over the world from zoom 7 out, the same sky in every browser: clumps gathered in patches, drifting with the world's one wind, over the fog's haze and under every sign. **In the clouds** from 7 to 6, **Over the clouds** from 6 out, gone by 3.5. A way of looking, not anything a house sees or does. See decision record 0098. `createCloudLayer`, `cloudBandAt`, `carried` |
| **Command** | A request to change the world, named as an imperative: `FoundHouse`. It may be rejected. Commands are not stored. |
| **Compass** | A compass rose - four cardinal points filled and four between them drawn, inside a ring: the way to everything that is not on the screen. On a phone it is a disc standing in the **Stage**'s bottom right corner, the one thing that lies over a **Bay** rather than sitting in one, and one tap puts **Navigation** in the bay under the thumb - the bottom one on a tall phone, the right one on a wide one; pressing it again gives that bay back. It **never resizes anything**, and what is under it steps clear rather than being covered. It rode the seam between the bays until 0047, which is the one place on the stage that moves. In the **Spread** it is a tab in the corner instead. It replaced a kebab that opened a menu over the world, because a menu that covers what it is about has to be dismissed to be used. See decision records 0043 and 0047. `Compass`, and `NEAR_BAY` is which bay it opens |
| **Component library** | Every part the interface is built from - containers, controls, readouts, collections - in `apps/web/src/ui/`, importing nothing from the game, and shown in every variant and state on its own page, `/admin/inspect-ui-components`. A component is seen there before it is used. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md` and decision record 0068. `src/ui/` |
| **Container** | Any boxed thing in the interface - a **Pane**, a card, a dialog, a tooltip - as four layers: a **Border**, a **Fill**, none or several **Embellishment**s, and the content. Named by a **Recipe**. A container in a container is two containers. See decision record 0068. `Container` |
| **Content** | What is in a **Slot**: a **Character** or an **Item**. A character is in one slot at a time, and stands at the structure's place. Not what the game is made of, its words and numbers, which are kept in **Catalogue**s. See decision record 0093. Not yet in the source |
| **Copy** | Land of an edited master that came from the same place on Earth as a larger piece did: a paste, or the Clone Stamp. **Invented land** that looks like somewhere, because every real place lands in the world at most once. Orange striped under the Origin renderer. See the **Origin map**. `ORIGIN_CLONE`, `markClones` |
| **Correlation id** | The id of the story an event belongs to. The first message of a story is its own correlation, and everything that follows from it carries the same; the server decides which story a command continues. On every event's envelope as the `correlationid` extension, and the debug panel tags each event line with it. See decision record 0035. `StoredEvent.correlationId` |
| **Critter** | A small wild animal the browser makes up from where and when: birds out of a bush, a hare, a fish jumping. The same in every browser, a look and nothing more: never sent, never stored, and nothing in the world knows of it. Drawn from a strip of frames a kind, `world/sprites/critters/`, which `world:critters` cuts; the kinds and their frames are `world/critters.json`. Only where the house sees now, from zoom 9 in. Adam's word. See decision record 0099. `CritterKind`, `critterFrameKey`, `eventsAt` |
| **Crossed out** | How a **Lost node** of a **Quest** is drawn: struck through, and shown for what it is even if it was a question mark, so the player sees what the road not taken held. See decision record 0096. Not yet in the source |
| **Culture** | The building tradition a **Structure** is drawn in: European, Islamic, East Asian or African so far, ground of any other borrowing the nearest. **The ground's, not the stone's**: each master has a culture map, worked out from its **Origin map** and a few places on Earth in `world/cultures.json`, and the page reads it under a structure when it draws one, so a castle keeps its look when it changes hands and a repainted master redraws it. Chooses the structure's picture and its word: *cathedral*, *great mosque*. See decision record 0093. Not yet in the source |
| **Decider** | The pure pair of functions that make up an aggregate: decide turns a command and the current state into events, evolve folds an event into the state. It holds the rules of the game and depends on nothing. One shape for every aggregate, so the loop that reads, folds, decides and appends is written once. `Decider`, and `HouseDecider` is the house's |
| **Delta** | An incremental change pushed to a client after its snapshot: a structure raised or changed, a person arrived, moved or gone. Never a house, which is not drawn on the map. |
| **Debug panel** | Adam's space down the right-hand side of the window: everything that helps while the game is being built, in one docked column, and nothing a player will ever have. It takes room from the **Stage** rather than floating over it, so narrowing it restricts the play area as a smaller screen would; a toggle in its top corner confines the stage to a phone. It minimises to a badge and remembers that. Which world is drawn and what draws it are part of it. **Only in a development build**: a production build leaves it out whole, and the stage has the window. See decision records 0023, 0039 and 0079. `DebugPanel`, brought in by `debug/devTools.ts`, and `stageBox.ts` is the arithmetic |
| **Deliver** | An **Objective** the player meets by handing over an amount of a resource: pressing *Deliver* spends it, and it is refused while the house has not that much **Free**. A resource objective is handed over rather than ticking itself, because a **Stock** grows with nothing written down and no event marks the moment it passes a number. See decision record 0096. `Objective.Deliver`, one of `Objective.MetByThePlayer` |
| **Desk** | The surface the map lies on: seen once the camera is pulled back past the fit zoom, where the sheet stops covering the window, and hidden above it. It has edges, because something can see past them - pull back past the floor and it stands in a **Room**. A drawn grain for now; later the player's workbench, with the house's papers on it. See decision records 0033 and experiment 0009. `Desk`, `deskOnScreen`, and `floorZoom` says how far back the camera may go |
| **Dialogue** | One conversation offered to a house, recorded whole when it is offered: a title, a **Scene**, its speakers, each in a **Party**, every **Line** and every **Dialogue choice**, and its **Requirement**s, so the roads not taken are history too, as a **Choice**'s are. A house can have several waiting at once, and keeps every one it has finished. What the player meets it as is **Character dialogue**. See decision record 0071. `Dialogue`, offered by `OfferDialogue` as `PlayerOfferedDialogue`, and `House.Founded.openDialogues` is what a house has open; `PlayerCompletedDialogue` is one reaching its end |
| **Dialogue choice** | The point in a **Dialogue** where the player picks what is said or done next instead of pressing *continue*: two to four options, each a **Line**, written when the dialogue is offered. **Not a Choice roll**, though the two are alike: some are plain choices of what to say, some will one day carry a roll's gamble, and what one offers can be shaped by the world. Taking an option is a **Dialogue step**. See decision record 0071. `Then.DialogueChoice` |
| **Dialogue step** | What a house's history records of a **Dialogue** being read: an option of a **Dialogue choice** taken, or a **Line** reached that has an effect, is marked to be recorded, or ends the dialogue. The plain lines between are read in the browser and never written down, so a conversation is a handful of lines in the events log rather than one a press, and a dialogue reopened picks up at its last step. What a step does follows it as the world's own events, from the same decision. See decision record 0071. `CompleteDialogueStep`, sent from the page as `completeDialogueStep` and recorded as `PlayerCompletedDialogueStep`; `Dialogue.isStep` says which lines are steps, and `Dialogue.walk` rebuilds the rest from them |
| **Diamond** | One cell of the grid the ground's colour is read on, at its finest: a square of world space one world unit across, turned as far as the ground's grid is turned (26 degrees for a player), which the tipped camera draws as a diamond. As wide as a **Master pixel**; away from the equator Mercator draws a master pixel taller than it is wide, so at Mordaunt's latitude one is about one and a half diamonds tall. What an **Area** is fenced on and what its **Terrain census** counts, whatever size the ground's cells are painted at. See decision records 0060 and 0066. `DiamondGrid`, `diamondAt`, and `lattice.ts` holds the arithmetic the painter and the fence share |
| **Dialog** | A question that has to be answered before anything else - kill this **Key figure**? - over the whole page, the page dimmed behind it and out of reach until it is answered or put away. What cannot be undone in one is answered by **Hold to confirm**. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md`. `Dialog` |
| **Dive** | How the loop turns over, and it turns over inside the mark for your own house. The camera is pinned at the closest zoom over the seat and the whole scene is scaled up about the middle of the castle until its pixels are the size of the window; the **Room** is inside them, growing out as you go in and shrinking back as you come out. By the time it is the window it is the same room at the same size the real one is about to be, so the camera can be moved underneath and not be seen doing it. It runs on a rail rather than on the wheel, because a passage is a thing that finishes. Only over your own holding, and only with a house to have one. See experiment 0009. `Dive`, `attachPull`, and `dive.ts` is the arithmetic |
| **Divider** | A painted rule between two things, as the moodboard draws them between its sections: a line laid between its two ends at whatever width it is given, with a **Knot** in its middle or a section's name on it. Adam's, in `world/ui/dividers/`, cut only at its two ends. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md` and `AUTHORING-THE-ART.md`. `Divider`, `Rule` |
| **Edge** | What joins one node of a **Quest** to the next: the quest moves along one when the node it leaves is done. An edge out of a dialogue node names the endings of its **Dialogue** that lead along it. Drawn walked, ahead or lost, and as a short stub where the node it leads to is not drawn. See decision record 0096. `Edge`, with `endings`; how it is drawn is `QuestWalk.stateOf` |
| **Division** | How the field of a banner is partitioned: plain, per pale, per fess, quarterly. `Division` |
| **Domain** | A house's reach over land and what it yields: a resource that **gates** rather than being spent. A house needs enough of it to attempt a thing, and attempting the thing does not use it up. **Domain is the word the player sees; the code says `NATURAL_DOMAIN`**, because `domain` already names the innermost layer of the server and one word for two things is a word nobody can grep for. `Resource.NATURAL_DOMAIN` |
| **Effect** | What taking an option does, carried on the option itself so that a choice is self-describing, and what a **Line** of a **Dialogue** does. Five kinds so far: one founds the house at a seat with a name and a banner, one grants the house some of a resource, one spends some of it, which is refused when the house has not that much free, one begins a conversation, and one pledges some of a resource until a day, which is what a quest's pledge costs (0096). What lasts while a structure is held is a **Standing effect** instead. The decider turns the taken option's effect, or the line's, into events in the same decision as the action or the step. `Effect`, with `Effect.FoundAt`, `Effect.Grant`, `Effect.Spend`, `Effect.Converse` and `Effect.Pledge` |
| **Embellishment** | A picture pinned to a **Container**'s corner or the middle of one of its edges - a cherub, a drape, a cross - which is not part of the **Border**. It says its **Overhang**, and takes none of the content's room: it stands where it is pinned, over the border or out past it. Adam's, in `world/ui/embellishments/`. Not the **Ornament**, which is drawn in code and is being replaced by these. See decision record 0068. `Embellishment` |
| **Enlightenment** | The age the world is in: reason spreading through instruments, presses, academies and letters, growing up through what came before rather than replacing it - castles still stand and a cathedral is still the largest thing for a hundred miles. Interrupted by the **Apocalypse**. The world opened on the first of January, 1790, in Earth's own years. See `SETTING.md`. Not yet in the source. |
| **Event** | Something that happened, named in the past tense: `HouseFounded`. Events are the only durable state and are never edited or deleted. Every one is either the world's or the player's; see the two rows below. Where one is kept is a separate question, answered by its stream: a house's are `HouseEvent`s. `Event` |
| **Event of the player** | Something a player did, by which the world was reached into: being asked something, and answering it. The mechanics of play rather than a happening in the world, recorded all the same because they are how everything in the world came to happen. `villages.player.<verb>.v<n>`, and `PlayerEvent` in the code. A small set on purpose. See decision record 0034. |
| **Event of the world** | Something that happened in the world: a thing came to be, moved, changed or ended. What the chronicle is made of and what the map is drawn from. `villages.house.<verb>.v<n>`, and `WorldEvent` in the code. The half that grows. See decision record 0034. |
| **Event family** | The events of one mechanic on a house's stream: what it has, what it is asked, what it is told, whom it holds, what it sees. The third thing said of an event, after where it is kept and which half it is in; the founding is in none, because every mechanic reads it. A fold with nothing to say about a mechanic passes its family in one branch. See decision record 0075. `ResourceEvent`, `ChoiceEvent`, `DialogueEvent`, `KeyFigureEvent`, `VisionEvent` |
| **Event id** | The identity of one event: the CloudEvent `id`, a UUID minted when it is written. What a consumer deduplicates by. See decision records 0024 and 0035. |
| **Event store** | The append-only record of every event, one stream per aggregate. MongoDB, through Occurrent, in a database of the worktree's own when Docker is running, and carried from there by the **Bridge** to the **Broker**; Occurrent's in-memory store when it is not. See decision records 0092 and 0094. |
| **Face** | The picture a button is drawn on, in `world/ui/controls/`: stretched between its end caps to fit its words when it has a file of cuts beside it, as a drape or an oval does, and laid whole when not, as a round one is. A round face may have its mark painted in or be plain for a drawn one. Its hovered, pressed and disabled states are light and shade over it unless they are painted as pictures of their own. See `UI-COMPONENTS.md` and `AUTHORING-THE-ART.md`. `faceOf` |
| **Fate engine** | The part of the game that rolls what can happen to a house at a moment: it works out the **Omen**s, rolls a **Rarity** from fixed odds, opens the **Pool**s the omens point at and deals a hand of **Action**s from them. The engine behind a **Choice roll**, and architecture rather than a mechanic: see `ARCHITECTURE.md` and decision record 0083. Its luck is the application's, never the domain's. Called the chrono-engine until 2026-09-19, when the chrono jump it was named for was dropped. |
| **Fault** | Something acting on its own that threw: a subscriber to the feed on an event, a listener at its work, or something scheduled for later. The feed says so and goes on without handing it that event again, so the read model is missing it and the fault is how anyone finds out: the server's log gets the stack, and every window's debug panel the one line, as an `error` frame with the code `fault`. See decision record 0029. `Fault`, reported to and watched on the `Faults` port |
| **Fence** | The boundary of an area: closed rings on the lattice between **Diamonds**, so every edge runs along the ground's own grid and a diamond is inside or outside and never both.
