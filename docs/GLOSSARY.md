# Glossary

> **What belongs here:** every word the project uses for a thing, and what it means. **What does not:** the
> reasoning behind any of them, which is in the decision records, and anything that is not a word.

The shared vocabulary of Villages. These words are used verbatim in code, in event names and in the interface. If a
term changes here, it changes in all three places in the same commit. Names in `code font` are identifiers that exist
in the source.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Aggregate** | A consistency boundary with its own event stream. `House` is the only one so far. |
| **Action** | Taking one option of the choice pending on a house: the house's answer, recorded as an event, with the option's effect following from the same decision. See decision record 0034. `TakeAction`, `PlayerTookAction` |
| **Area** | A closed region of the world: a fence, drawn as a line with an optional shade inside, which knows the terrain census of what it encloses. The shape an influence zone is drawn as, and the component anything else fenced reuses. See decision record 0032. `Area`, `Fence`, and `enclose` then `fenceAround` make one from a drawn path |
| **Arrival** | A player saying hello. A player with no house and no choice pending is given the founding choice on arrival; a player with either is given nothing. `Arrival`, and `FoundingChoices` writes the words |
| **Arrangement** | How the two **Bay**s of the **Bays** divide the **Stage** between them, as one value with three settings: a **Strip** and the rest, half each, or the rest and a strip. **A property of the fold and not of the panes in it** - a bay is one end of a stage two things are sharing, and what is standing in it has nothing to say about how much of it there is. That is what makes the three sizes mean the same thing on every pane, and what stops the **Compass** resizing anything. Both bays put away is not an arrangement, so it is not a state a player can reach. Named by the **Pane size control** on either bay. See decision record 0046. `Arrangement`, and `askedIn` says which size each bay is drawn at |
| **Banner** | A house's heraldic device: a field of one or two tinctures, a division, and a charge. The visible identity of a house, shown in the interface and as its sprite on the map. `Banner` |
| **Bay** | Where a **Pane** sits: a rectangle the layout hands out, in both of the **Stage**'s forms. More than one pane can share one, taking turns behind a **Tab bar**. In the **Spread** they are the **Floor** and three **Tile**s on it; in the **Bays** they are the two boxes the **Stage** splits into once it is too small to show everything at once, divided along the stage's longer dimension, so a portrait stage gives a top and a bottom. How much of the stage each of those two gets is the **Arrangement**, which belongs to the fold rather than to whichever pane is standing in it. Distinct from the **Debug panel**, which is Adam's and is not on the stage at all. See decision records 0040 and 0046. `bayLayout` is the arithmetic and `bays.ts` is the manager |
| **Bays** | The **Stage**'s form when there is no longer room for everything at once: two **Bay**s cut along the stage's longer side, so a portrait stage gives a top and a bottom and a landscape one gives a left and a right. What it is at or below 600 pixels on its shorter side; above 660 it is the **Spread**, and between the two it keeps whichever it had. A **Pane** in it has **six states** - three sizes, each upright and turned - and the player says which of the three **Arrangement**s the two bays are in. See decision records 0040, 0041 and 0046. `twoBays`, and `formFor` says which form the stage is in |
| **Carousel** | One thing at a time, with a step either side and a dot per thing beneath: the small form of a **Pane** whose full form is a list, so the list can be glanced through in a strip of screen while the map stays up beside it. The **Choice roll**'s small size, and its only use so far. See decision record 0044. `Carousel` in `Choosing.tsx` |
| **Causation id** | The id of the message that directly caused an event: for an event from a command, the command's id, which the debug log carries because commands are not stored. On every event's envelope as the `causationid` extension, and on the `event` frame. See decision record 0035. `StoredEvent.causationId` |
| **Cell** | A square of the world used to partition live updates and map queries: a quadkey at any zoom from 0 to 10, so a cell's ancestor is its prefix. Derived from a place wherever it is needed, never carried on an event. See decision record 0026. `Cell` |
| **Charge** | The figure borne on a banner, such as a tower, a stag or a star. `Charge` |
| **Choice** | A prompt and two to four options put to a house at a moment. It can expire: a choice nobody answers stops being relevant once the world has moved on. Shown to the player as a parchment. See decision record 0034. `Choice`, `GiveChoice`, `PlayerGivenChoice`, and `House.pending` is the one a house has |
| **Character dialogue** | The mechanic people talk to each other in: speech bubbles between characters standing on either side, stepped through by the player, covering a conversation, a monologue and a group discussion alike. What storylines are carried in, and a place a **Choice roll** can open. See `MECHANICS.md`. Not yet in the source. |
| **Choice roll** | The mechanic a choice is offered by: the game rolls a few things that could happen from the state the house is in, and the player picks one. A player can provoke one, and the world can raise one unprompted inside an event or a storyline. See `MECHANICS.md`; the engine behind it is the **Fate engine**. A **Pane** with three sizes - the options laid out, the **Carousel**, and set aside to a strip - and the one pane whose size is the player's rather than the layout's. See decision record 0044. |
| **Command** | A request to change the world, named as an imperative: `FoundHouse`. It may be rejected. Commands are not stored. |
| **Compass** | A compass rose - four cardinal points filled and four between them drawn, inside a ring: the way to everything that is not on the screen. On a phone it is a disc standing in the **Stage**'s bottom right corner, the one thing that lies over a **Bay** rather than sitting in one, and one tap puts **Navigation** in the bay under the thumb - the bottom one on a tall phone, the right one on a wide one; pressing it again gives that bay back. It **never resizes anything**, and what is under it steps clear rather than being covered. It rode the seam between the bays until 0047, which is the one place on the stage that moves. In the **Spread** it is a tab in the corner instead. It replaced a kebab that opened a menu over the world, because a menu that covers what it is about has to be dismissed to be used. See decision records 0043 and 0047. `Compass`, and `NEAR_BAY` is which bay it opens |
| **Correlation id** | The id of the story an event belongs to. The first message of a story is its own correlation, and everything that follows from it carries the same; the server decides which story a command continues. On every event's envelope as the `correlationid` extension, and the debug panel tags each event line with it. See decision record 0035. `StoredEvent.correlationId` |
| **Decider** | The pure pair of functions that make up an aggregate: decide turns a command and the current state into events, evolve folds an event into the state. It holds the rules of the game and depends on nothing. `HouseDecider` |
| **Delta** | An incremental change pushed to a client after its snapshot: a house appeared, or a house changed. |
| **Debug panel** | Adam's space down the right-hand side of the window: everything that helps while the game is being built, in one docked column, and nothing a player will ever have. It takes room from the **Stage** rather than floating over it, so narrowing it restricts the play area as a smaller screen would; a toggle in its top corner confines the stage to a phone. It minimises to a badge and remembers that. Which world is drawn and what draws it are part of it. See decision records 0023 and 0039. `DebugPanel`, and `stageBox.ts` is the arithmetic |
| **Desk** | The surface the map lies on: seen once the camera is pulled back past the fit zoom, where the sheet stops covering the window, and hidden above it. A drawn grain for now; later the player's workbench, with the house's papers on it. See decision record 0033. `Desk`, and `floorZoom` says how far back the camera may go |
| **Division** | How the field of a banner is partitioned: plain, per pale, per fess, quarterly. `Division` |
| **Effect** | What taking an option does, carried on the option itself so that a choice is self-describing. Today the only kind founds the house at a seat with a name and a banner. The decider turns the taken option's effect into events in the same decision as the action. `Effect`, and `Effect.FoundAt` is the only kind so far |
| **Event** | Something that happened, named in the past tense: `HouseFounded`. Events are the only durable state and are never edited or deleted. Every one is either the world's or the player's; see the two rows below. |
| **Event of the player** | Something a player did, by which the world was reached into: being asked something, and answering it. The mechanics of play rather than a happening in the world, recorded all the same because they are how everything in the world came to happen. `villages.player.<verb>.v<n>`, and `PlayerEvent` in the code. A small set on purpose. See decision record 0034. |
| **Event of the world** | Something that happened in the world: a thing came to be, moved, changed or ended. What the chronicle is made of and what the map is drawn from. `villages.house.<verb>.v<n>`, and `WorldEvent` in the code. The half that grows. See decision record 0034. |
| **Event id** | The identity of one event: the CloudEvent `id`, a UUID minted when it is written. What a consumer deduplicates by. See decision records 0024 and 0035. |
| **Event store** | The append-only record of every event, one stream per aggregate. MongoDB, through Occurrent. |
| **Eye** | The player's switch for keeping their own holdings in sight: with it open, their seat is drawn however far back the camera goes and the ground about it below the zoom where anyone else's starts. Theirs and nobody else's, so the world still thins out with distance. Costs nothing on the wire, because a player's own house already reaches them wherever they are looking. The first place in the bar along the bottom of the house card. Distinct from **Seeing everything**. See decision record 0038. `eyeIsOpen`, and `always` on the sprite |
| **Fate engine** | The part of the game that rolls what can happen to a house at a moment, from its stats, its spirit, where the player is looking and what is around. The engine behind a **Choice roll**, and architecture rather than a mechanic: see `ARCHITECTURE.md`. Not yet in the source. Called the chrono-engine until 2026-09-19, when the chrono jump it was named for was dropped. |
| **Fence** | The boundary of an area: closed rings on the lattice between master pixels, so every edge is axis-aligned and a pixel is inside or outside and never both. Two areas drawn flush share their boundary exactly. Traced from the pixels a drawn path took, by the same walk a coastline is traced with. See decision record 0032. `Fence` |
| **Floor** | The **Bay** that is the whole **Stage**, with every **Tile** lying on it: the map, in the **Spread**. The one bay exempt from the rule that bays never overlap, because everything overlaps it on purpose - which is what makes the map the ground the interface stands on rather than a pane competing with the rest for room. The **Bays** have no floor: there the map is a pane like any other. See decision record 0041. `bays.floor` in `bayLayout` |
| **Frame** | One message on the wire between the gateway and the browser: JSON text with a `type` field naming it. The fixtures in `contracts/fixtures/frames` own the format, and both ends are tested against them. `ClientFrame`, `ServerFrame` |
| **Gateway** | The service that holds player connections, serves snapshots and pushes deltas. Stateless apart from its live connections. |
| **Gazetteer** | The hand-kept list of named seats the founding choice draws from, in the server, until the server knows terrain. Every seat in it has been read out of the zoom 7 tiles of every master and is on land in all of them. `Gazetteer` |
| **Holding** | What a house has on the map rather than in its name: today its seat and the ground about it, and later the buildings and works it raises. What the **Eye** keeps in sight. |
| **House** | The thing a player plays: a noble lineage with a name, a banner, a seat and a treasury. One event stream each, and one house a player - its identity is worked out from the player's, so founding twice is refused. `House` |
| **House status** | The mechanic that says how your own house stands: who it is, what it has and what it can do, always on screen and never opened. Carries the bar of the house's own levers, of which the **Eye** is the first. Distinct from an **Inspect window**, which is opened on a thing and can be about anybody's house. See `MECHANICS.md`. `HouseStatus`, and `HouseBar` is the bar. |
| **House-spirit** | The character a house has earned through everything it has done, as traits and effects that colour its rolls. Mostly a permanent progression: a house taken down by the world keeps what its spirit earned. Not yet in the source. |
| **Influence zone** | A geographic area a house has some kind of reach over, drawn on the map as a fence. Owned or controlled territory is the common kind. The first is the ground about a seat, a fixed reach in master pixels, worked out from the seat rather than stored. Drawn as a `Fence` like any area, by `influenceZoneAround`, which is why it is counted and read by the same code. |
| **Inspect window** | The mechanic that says what one thing in the world is: a window on a **Subject**, with what the world knows about it and the **Links** worth following on from it. Not the map's - a **Pane** anything can open, and the map is only the first place a subject is pointed at. See `MECHANICS.md`. Called the inspector until 2026-09-19, when it was named, given links and made to hold more than a house. `InspectWindow`, `describe.ts` turns a subject into what it shows, and `subjects.ts` holds what is open. |
| **Key figure** | One of the few named members of a house the player acts through. A house has uncounted members behind them, so losing every key figure is a low power state, never a loss. Not yet in the source. |
| **Link** | A way on from an **Inspect window**: either another **Subject**, which opens a window on it, or a **Pane**, which brings that pane up on this subject - *show it on the map* is the first of those. **One button, opening in the bay the window it came from is not in**, so the window stays on the screen; a player who wants it elsewhere has **Navigation**. *Show it on the map* is drawn as the map's own mark rather than as words, in both of the **Stage**'s forms, because it is the only link that opens no window. See `MECHANICS.md` and decision record 0049. `Link` in `inspect/describe.ts` |
| **Master pixel** | One pixel of `world/master/terrain.png`, the image the world is painted in by hand: one terrain class, about 1.2 kilometres at the equator, and the unit an area is counted in. Read back out of the zoom 7 tiles, which carry them exactly. `MasterGrid` |
| **Minimise** | Collapsing a **Pane** to a **Strip**, so the room it had goes to whatever is beside it. A property of a pane everywhere rather than of the small screen: it works in the **Spread** too, which is how a player who wants the map gets the map. **Every pane can be**, the map included since 0046 - the map's strip says *Map* and pressing it is the way back. Not a case the layout arithmetic knows about: a minimised bay asks for a smaller number than a half one does. See decision records 0040 and 0046. `askFor`, with the size `strip` |
| **Navigation** | The **Pane** that is the way on and the way back, called **Go to** on its own chrome: the fixed panes above, and the **Trail** of what has been looked at below. Reached in the **Spread** through the **Compass** tab in the corner it shares with the **House status**, and on a phone by pressing the compass, which puts it in the bay under the thumb. Every row carries a **Needle** in the bays and none in the spread, so any pane can be put in either bay: no pane is privileged, and which bay two panes share on a desktop says nothing about where either can go on a phone. It lists the trail and nothing else in the spread, because everything a player might navigate *to* is already on the screen there. Put away, its **Strip** carries the marks of the panes it could move, which is what lets the compass land in it. See decision records 0042, 0046 and 0047. `Navigating`, and `NAVIGATION_PANE` declares it |
| **Needle** | The one mark that means *there*, pointing at a **Bay**. One inside a ring is the **Compass**. One drawing turned four ways, so a player learns it once. A pair of them was **the arrows**, which said which bay a thing opened into; nothing draws a pair any more, and a row of **Navigation** carries a single needle to the other bay. See `LOOK-AND-FEEL.md` and decision records 0043 and 0049. `Needle` in `Icons.tsx` |
| **Option** | One of the two to four things a choice offers: a label, a line of detail, and its effect. `Option` |
| **Ornament** | How the interface embellishes an edge, in one vocabulary wherever it does: nested contours at decreasing weight rather than one thick line, a **stud** where something is fixed, a lozenge where two runs meet, and an open curl that springs away and turns back without closing. All of it gold on parchment, and **none of it carries meaning** - the heraldry is the field and the charge, and this is the frame they hang in. Drawn for the achievement the shield hangs in and shared with the **Seam**. See decision record 0048 and `LOOK-AND-FEEL.md`. `.ornament`, `.ornament__stud` |
| **Pane** | A thing that can occupy a **Bay**: the map, the **House status**, **Navigation**, the **Choice roll**, an **Inspect window**, and **Character dialogue** when it comes. Declared once - what it is called, the mark it is known by, which **Tile** of the **Spread** it belongs in and how tall that tile is - and then placed by the layout, which knows nothing of what any of them are. It declares nothing about a phone, because there the **Arrangement** decides. A pane has three sizes and owns all three, which on a phone is **six states**: each size upright and the same size turned. See decision records 0040 and 0046. `Pane`, and `panesFor` says what there is |
| **Pane size control** | The three marks in a **Pane**'s corner saying how much of the **Stage** it is taking: put away, the normal size, opened out. On every pane and in both of the stage's forms, bar the map, whose **Strip** is its own way back and which carries no control. In the **Bays** it names the **Arrangement**, and the other bay follows because both of them read one value; in the **Spread** each **Tile**'s height is its own. The mark is how much of a box is filled, which is the thing being chosen rather than a picture of it. See decision records 0045 and 0046 and `LOOK-AND-FEEL.md`. `PaneSizeControl` |
| **Parchment** | How a choice is put to the player: a sheet lying over the map with the prompt and one button an option, which folds to a corner and is gone once the choice is taken. `Parchment` |
| **Player** | The human. Identified for now by an anonymous token generated in the browser, and playing from one place at a time. `PlayerId` |
| **Player-time** | The order things were actually done in at a keyboard: the event store's own order. What arbitration between players runs on. Distinct from world-time. On every event as `recordedAt`, the server's instant. |
| **Projection** | A read model built by folding events, kept in MongoDB and queried by the interface. The event store is never queried to draw the map. |
| **Provenance** | What a command brings so that its events can say where they came from: the command's id and the correlation id of the story it continues, stamped on the envelope by the executor. See decision record 0035. `Provenance` |
| **Rarity** | How good a rolled option is, in four bands: common, uncommon, rare and legendary, shown as gray, green, blue and orange. A choice roll offers options of comparable value, so a legendary never sits beside two commons. See `LOOK-AND-FEEL.md`. Not yet in the source. |
| **Rate** | A resource expressed as an amount, a rate per second, and the instant at which that was true. Clients interpolate between updates, so the world needs no tick events. |
| **Restart** | A player starting over: the browser forgets its player token and reloads as a new player, who is given the founding choice again. The old house stays in the world; nothing is deleted. A debug panel button. See decision record 0036. |
| **Seat** | Where a house is founded: its place on the map, and the anchor of its sprite. `Location` |
| **Seam** | The join between the two **Bay**s of the **Bays**, and the moulding drawn over it: three gold rules running the length of the **Stage** - a heavy one on the join and a lighter one either side - with a fixed knot of **Ornament** at the middle, in the same vocabulary as the frame the shield hangs in. It **lies over** the two bays rather than taking room from them, so no layout arithmetic knows it exists, and it takes no pointer. The knot is one size whatever the length, because an ornament that stretches is an ornament that has been noticed. One drawing turned: a band across a portrait stage and the same band down a landscape one. The **Compass** stands on it. See decision record 0048. `Seam`, and `--bay-first` is where it is |
| **Seeing everything** | Adam's switch, in the debug panel, for looking at a whole world at once: significance is set aside on this connection, so every house in a held cell is sent however far back the camera is, and the renderer's distance floor comes off with it. Not a player's, because a world where everything is drawn from orbit is a world with no distance in it. See decision record 0038. `seeAll` on the wire, and `inCell(everything = true)` in the read model |
| **Session** | One live connection between a player and the gateway. A player has one: opening a second window takes the player over and stands the first down. What it holds - its cells, the day it is looking at - is its own; what is the player's is the same in whichever window they are in. See decision record 0028. |
| **Sheet** | The world's square seen as one object: what the camera shows once it is pulled back past the fit zoom, where the sheet stops covering the window and the desk shows around it. Above the fit zoom the window stays inside the sheet, as it always did; below it the sheet stays inside the window, down to a floor where it is half the window's shorter side. One camera does both. See decision record 0033. `sheetOnScreen`, `fitZoom`, and `constrainCamera` is the rule |
| **Significance** | How much a thing matters to the world, from nothing to a hundred, carried on the event that made it and deciding how far out on the map it stays drawn. A hundred is drawn from the whole world. `Significance`, and `coarsestZoom` turns it into a zoom |
| **Snapshot** | The current contents of a cell, sent when a client subscribes to it, before any deltas. |
| **Spread** | The **Stage**'s form when there is room for everything at once: the map filling it, the **House status** in a corner, and any **Inspect window** in a rail beside them. What it is above 600 pixels on its shorter side; below that it is the **Bays**. See decision record 0040. `FOLDS_UNDER`, and `bayLayout` says which form the stage is in |
| **Stage** | The box the player's interface is laid out in: the map, the **Desk**, the overlays, the **House status**, any **Inspect window** and the **Parchment**, and nothing of Adam's. It is the window minus the **Debug panel**, and it is resized by two grips or confined to a phone, which is how a mechanic is checked at a shape there is no monitor for. It has two forms, the **Spread** and the **Bays**. See decision records 0039 and 0040. `.stage`, and `stageBox` says how big it is |
| **Stream** | The ordered events of one aggregate instance, addressed as `house:<uuid>`. |
| **Strip** | A **Pane** put away: a band 44 pixels across with the pane's name on it and the **Pane size control** beside it, and unless the pane says otherwise that is the whole of it. The same band turned a quarter in landscape, 44 pixels wide down the side of the stage. One shape and one thickness for every pane, so that putting anything away looks like putting anything else away. Two panes say otherwise: the map's strip carries no control and is itself the way back, and **Navigation**'s carries the marks of what it could put in the other bay, because that is the strip a player arrives in by pressing the **Compass**. What **Minimise** produces. See decision records 0046 and 0047. `STRIP` is how many pixels |
| **Subject** | The one thing an **Inspect window** is about: a house or an area today, and later anything the world grows that can be pointed at. A tagged identity rather than a bare id, so that one window can render every kind of thing the way one **Parchment** renders every **Choice**. One store for both kinds, replacing the two selections that used to run side by side. `Subject`, and `subjects.ts` holds what is open |
| **Tab bar** | Several **Pane**s sharing one **Bay** and taking turns in it, with a strip of marks across its top saying which is up. The panes declare that they share by naming the same bay; the layout never decides it, so a tab cannot appear and vanish as the window is dragged. Whether panes sharing a bay take turns or **stack** is the bay's own property - the rail stacks its windows and never grows tabs. Marks and no words, because a column is 26rem at its widest. Only in the **Spread**. See decision record 0042 and `LOOK-AND-FEEL.md`. `TabBar`, and `showingIn` says which is up |
| **Terrain census** | How many master pixels of each terrain class an area encloses. Derived from the fence whenever it is asked for rather than stored, so a repainted world or a moved edge means a fresh answer. `Census`, and `censusOf` takes one by reading the pixels out of the tiles. |
| **Tile** | A **Bay** of the **Spread** that is not the **Floor**: the column's rail and corner, and the one along the bottom middle. **Tiles never overlap each other and none leaves the stage**, which is a test over every size the spread can be rather than a claim - it is why the layout is arithmetic and not a stylesheet. An empty tile takes no room at all. See decision record 0041. `TILES`, and `bayLayout` says where each one is |
| **Tincture** | A heraldic colour. Metals are or and argent; colours are gules, azure, sable, vert and purpure. A charge of a metal goes on a field of a colour, and the reverse. `Tincture` |
| **Trail** | What the player has looked at, newest first, twelve steps back: the whole content of **Navigation**'s Recent section. Distinct from what is *open*, which is three at most and forgotten as a window closes. **The only thing on the stage that survives a reload**, because a trail that empties itself is not a trail. A step keeps the name the thing was looked at under, since the trail outlives what it points at. See decision record 0042. `trail.ts`, and the window is what records a name |
| **Treasury** | The stored wealth of a house, expressed as a rate. The first resource in the world. `Treasury` |
| **Warp** | The pure transformation that turns a place on Earth into a place in our world: control points that drag their surroundings, a seeded wobble, and optionally a turn of the globe. Applied once, at build time, and configured by `world/warp.json`. |
| **Wipe** | Emptying the development world: every stream and every projection cleared, and every session told, so that each browser arrives anew. A debug panel button, and not the wipe of decision record 0011. See decision record 0036. |
| **World** | The single shared place every player is on: a warped Earth, generated by us and canonical. Its coordinates are longitude and latitude, and they are not Earth's. There are no shards. |
| **World-time** | The story's clock: a day in the world's calendar, which spans centuries, counted from the first day of year one. One world month passes for every real day, the same for everyone, and there is no fast-forward. What the chronicle and the map at a moment run on. It moves forward only; the game is played chronologically. Distinct from player-time. See decision record 0051. `WorldTime`; on every event as `worldTime`, and read as a date in Earth's own calendar by `Calendar` |
