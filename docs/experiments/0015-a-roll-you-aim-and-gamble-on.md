# 0015. A roll you aim and gamble on

- Status: open
- Started: 2026-10-04

## Hypothesis

**A choice roll whose omens are shown to the player, and whose options carry the marks of the omens that
brought them, feels both aimed and lucky: the player can steer what a hand is about by what they look at,
while its rarity stays a real gamble.** The roll's history is enough to say, for any hand, why it was what it
was; a new omen source or a new action costs only itself; and the machine deals a hand of one rarity every
time it is asked, whatever the omens.

It is wrong if a player, after a few dozen rolls, cannot say what any omen does to a hand; if aiming the roll
on purpose - looking at a river, focusing a key figure - makes no visible difference to what is dealt; if the
report card reads as a loading screen rather than a moment; if a hand is ever dealt short or mixed in rarity;
if a new omen source or a new action needs anything but itself and its test; or if the stream cannot answer
"why was I offered this?".

## Setup

The build in `docs/plans/choice-roll.md`, shaped by decision records 0083 and 0084: a Take action button that
costs ten wealth; omens from the house, its changes, what the page has open, the ground in the looking circle
and the season; fixed odds; pools that only tilt the draw; twenty resource actions and one gossip dialogue;
a report card that the omens slam into, a rarity stamp, and the hand.

As little as possible: one action per rarity per resource, a handful of omen sources, no world-raised roll, no
expiry, no hint on the button, no price but wealth.

## What we will look at

- **By eye, Adam playing**: whether he aims rolls on purpose after a while, and whether he can say what an
  omen did. Whether the stamp lands as a moment, and whether the report card has got tiresome by the
  fiftieth roll.
- **As tests**: a hand of one rarity, three options, every time; the odds over many rolls with seeded dice;
  an action in two open pools drawn about twice as often as one in one; a refused roll writing nothing.
- **The history**: for a roll picked at random from a long session, whether its event alone says which omens
  brought each option.
- **The cost of growth**: how many files the first omen source added after the plan touches, and the gossip
  action.
