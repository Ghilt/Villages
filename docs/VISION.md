# Vision

What the experience is. What the world is and how its time works is `SETTING.md`.

Villages is a persistent world in which each player is a fictional noble house on a map of the real Earth. You found a
seat, raise a banner, and watch your house accumulate what it accumulates. There is no campaign to complete. The
question the experience asks is not "did you win" but "what is your house, and what does it stand for".

## Pillars

**A house, not an avatar.** You do not play a person. You play a lineage that outlives any individual in it. Decisions
are taken in the house's name and remembered as the house's history, because that history is literally the event
stream the world is built from.

**Discovery over instruction.** There is no tutorial and no visible tech tree. You learn what your house is by looking
at it and by acting. Mechanics are revealed by use. A player should be able to sit with the map, the banner and a
ticker for a minute and feel that something is going on that is worth understanding.

**Identity is emergent, and it is yours.** The house has leanings that the world reveals to you over time, and your
decisions push them. The banner, the name and eventually the words of the house are the visible summary of that
identity, so two players who played the same opening should still end up with houses that read differently.

**A world that is shared and continuous.** One Earth, one world, everyone on it. It runs whether you are watching or
not. Other houses on the map are other people, and their marks on the world are the same kind of thing as yours.

**Presence, not pressure.** Resources are rates, not chores. Nothing decays because you slept. Absence costs you
tempo, never your house. Looking at the map should be pleasant on its own.

**A small surface with a deep reading.** The interface stays close to a banner, a few tickers, a map and the
occasional decision. Depth comes from interpretation and roleplay, not from menu density.

## Tone

Restrained and chronicle-like. The world reports rather than announces: short entries in the register of a chronicle,
not quest text. Heraldry is the visual language of tinctures, divisions and charges. The map is timeless rather than
modern, so the base style is stripped of highways and brand names.

## What it is not

- Not a conquest optimiser. If the dominant verb becomes "expand efficiently", we have taken a wrong turn.
- Not an idle clicker. Tickers exist to make time legible, not to be tapped.
- Not a simulation of medieval economics. Plausibility serves the fiction, not the reverse.
- Not player-versus-player first. Other houses are context and company before they are opposition.
- Not a strategy game with a map bolted on. The map is the main screen because the world is a place.

## How we will know it is working

- A new player, given no instructions, founds a house and stays with it for a few minutes.
- Two players describe their houses differently, in their own words, without prompting.
- Watching the world with nothing to do is still enjoyable.
- We can explain any change in the world by pointing at the events that caused it.

## Open questions

These are live and belong to the experiment journal, not to this document:

- What, beyond a seat, does a house hold on the map?
- Which resources exist, and what is the first decision worth making about them?
- How do houses become aware of one another, and what is the first thing they can do about it?
- What form do house leanings take, and how visibly should the world reflect them?
