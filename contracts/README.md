# Contracts

The wire format between the gateway and the browser, as checked-in JSON fixtures.

`fixtures/` holds one file per message type, plus vectors for the maths both sides must agree on, such as the mapping
from a location to a cell. The Kotlin `protocol` module and the TypeScript `protocol` package each have a test that
parses every fixture, round-trips it, and compares. Neither side owns the format: the fixtures do.

Add a fixture in the same change as the message type; both sides' tests fail without it.

The frames, under `fixtures/frames/client` and `fixtures/frames/server`: one frame per file, exactly as it crosses the
wire, nothing else in the file. Every frame has a `type` field naming it. Times are the server's clock in
milliseconds since the epoch, as numbers. Optional fields are left out when absent rather than written as null.
Identifiers are strings. Heraldry is spelled the way the browser shows it: `or`, `perPale`, `stag`.

`fixtures/cells.json` holds the vectors for the arithmetic from a location to a cell, generated by an implementation
independent of both sides. It also holds the coarsest zoom a thing of each significance is drawn from, worked out by
hand from the rule, which the gateway sends by. `fixtures/reach.json` does the same for whether a place lies within a vision point's reach,
which is what the gateway holds back by and what the page draws the fog clear inside, and `fixtures/coverage.json`
for several points together with the surface tension between them, generated by trying ball centres by brute force.
`fixtures/stands.json` holds a tile's stand records as the sprite map writes them and the `stands` frame carries
them, with what each decodes to, generated by the world package's own encoder. `fixtures/stand-tiles.json` says
which zoom 7 tiles' stands a held cell brings, which the gateway sends by and the page keeps by, worked out by hand.
`fixtures/names.json` holds a master's real names as `world:names` files them for the page and the game server, a
`names.json` and its `regions.bin` in base64, generated by the world package's own encoder and read by the server's
`FolderNames`. See decision record 0087.

## The versions of the wire

`protocol-v<n>.txt` is the shape of the wire at each version: every frame, and every field it carries however deep,
by its path. Both ends hold themselves to the recording of the version they declare, and a recorded version is never
recorded over. See decision record 0037. What each one changed:

- **2** - the first gameplay loop, which added `takeAction`, `yourChoice`, `wipeWorld` and `worldWiped`, and gave
  every event its provenance.
- **3** - `seeAll`, the eye that sets significance aside.
- **6** - the key figures: `yourKeyFigures`, and the commands that bring one in and act through one.
- **7** - put them on the map: the characters in a cell ride its snapshot beside the houses, three deltas say one
  arrived, changed or went, and two commands move one and take one off the map.
- **8** - gave a character on the map the birthday and the years an age is counted from, so a window on them can
  say how old they are. See decision record 0055.
- **9** - `yourSight`, the vision points a house sees from, which the fog of war is drawn from.
- **10** - `giveVision` and `takeVision`, a house given vision of a place outright and having it taken away. See
  decision record 0061.
- **11** - `fogOfWar`, Adam's switch, because the gateway now holds back what a house cannot see.
- **12** - gave `yourSight` its `tension`, the surface tension that joins circles close together. See decision
  record 0064.
- **13** - `stands`, the scenery's stands on ground a house has revealed, and gave `hello` the `master` the page
  draws, so the gateway knows whose stands to send. See decision record 0067.
- **14 to 19** are the dialogue's, built on a branch that had not met 13: they were 13 to 18 there, moved up one
  when main's landed first, so their recordings have no `stands`. **14** let an option's effect be a `grant`, some
  of a resource given, which is what a line of a dialogue gives too (decision record 0071). **15** added
  `offerDialogue`, the debug panel's offer of a sample dialogue by name. **16** added `yourDialogues`, the player's
  dialogues with what has not been reached held back, and `showRequirements`, Adam's switch that sent one window
  their requirements. **17** let an effect be a `spend`, what an option costs, and named what a said line did its
  `effects`. **18** gave an option its `revealImpact`, how it shows what taking it will do. **19** added
  `completeDialogueStep`, a step taken through one of the house's dialogues, and took away `showRequirements` and a
  dialogue's `requirements`: the debug panel only offers a dialogue now, so nothing asks for them, and a page is
  never sent them.
- **20** - the two together, the stands and the dialogue, as they met on main.
- **21** - gave a line still ahead its `shows`, what it shows as it is reached while playing: a place, a house or a
  character.
- **22** - no frame changed. The recording learned to see every field however deep, by its path, so a field added
  three objects down moves it as a frame does; before, only a frame's own fields were recorded, and 17 and 18
  differ from the versions before them by the number alone. The recordings before 22 keep the old form.
- **23** - the choice roll: `roll`, Take action with what the page is looking at; `nextRarity`, Adam's lever on
  the next roll's luck; `rollDrawn`, what a roll drew from, sent only while the instruments are open; and a choice's
  `omens` and an option's `rarity` and `omens`, left out of any choice no roll dealt. See decision record 0083.
- **24** - let an effect be a `converse`, a conversation begun by taking an option, said by the dialogue's
  `title` and nothing of what is said in it.
- **25** - `inspect`, a window asking after its subject wherever the camera is, and `inspected`, the one answer:
  the house or the character as the map shows them, or neither where the fog holds them back. See decision
  record 0088.
- **26** - gave `inspected` its `seenUntil`, the world-day the fog hid somebody a window asked after, who is
  answered as they were last seen. See decision record 0089.
- **27** - moved `seenUntil` onto what it dates: a house's view carries the world-day the fog hid it from the house
  it is sent to, from a cell, a change or a window's answer, and a character's view on `inspected` the same, so the
  page shows the day it is told and works none of it out. See decision record 0089's amendment.
- **28 to 31** are the waters', built on a branch that had not met 25: they were 25 to 28 there, moved up three
  when main's landed first, so their recordings have no `inspect`, and their answer to a click is named
  `inspected`, as it was there. **28** - a page asks what river it points at: `inspectAt`, a place clicked, and the
  answer, whether the house has revealed it and the river there by its id, its name and today's beside a name of
  1790, or none (decision record 0087). **29** - a click asks about any water, not only a river: the answer says
  the `water` there, a `river`, a `lake` or the `sea` by its `kind`. **30** - gave `roll` its `inspectingWaters`,
  the places the page has windows on water open at, which the server reads again for the water there: a roll reads
  a window on the Vistula as *Inspecting the Vistula*. **31** - gave the `water` its `about`, a line or two about
  the river, lake or sea from `world/names/about.json`, where one was written.
- **32** - the two together, the windows that ask after their subjects and the water a click points at, as they
  met on main: main's `inspected` keeps its name, and the answer to `inspectAt` is `inspectedAt`.
